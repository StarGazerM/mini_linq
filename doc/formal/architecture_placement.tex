\section{Where each physical choice belongs}
\label{sec:architecture-placement}
Physical compilation first records its decisions in Physical IR, then realizes
that fixed program. A macro alias selects a pass or policy; it does not replace
every relation's implementation.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.22\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.68\linewidth}}
\toprule
Boundary & Required decision or result \\
\midrule
RA planning & Fix operator occurrences and their exact operand versions. \\
IX construction & Define each required $(r,C)$ index and write its identity at
each admitted use position. \\
IX $\to$ Physical & A replaceable policy $\eta$ produces
$P=(\mathrm{IX},\Phi,\Lambda)$: relation/index bindings and a selected lowering
for every RA definition. \\
Physical $\to$\newline Rust[Access] & Dispatch through the recorded bindings and lowerings;
establish the compiled representations and views in $\Gamma$. \\
Access consumption & Preserve the resulting computation's meaning; execution
scheduling and push/pull placement remain open. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The proposed API aliases are \texttt{choose\_physical!} and
\texttt{realize\_physical!}. For example, imports can bind them to
\texttt{policies::given\_bindings} and \texttt{lowering::by\_bindings}; these
are direct aliases, not wrapper macros. Selection receives $\eta$;
realization receives the fixed $P$ and never invokes $\eta$ again.
The open environment $\Omega$ supplies provider and lowering definitions.
Dispatch resolves their recorded paths, not a built-in data-structure enum.

\paragraph{Representation and index compatibility.}
$\Phi_R(r)$ establishes the representation of relation binding $r$;
$\Phi_I(i)$ establishes the representation and access of index $i$.
$\Lambda(x)$ names the lowering of the RA definition producing $x$, including
\texttt{Rename}, \texttt{Unit} and other admitted definitions. External host
inputs receive relation bindings but no lowering entry: they are not RA
definitions. These choices must satisfy the selected definitions' construction
and use premises.
Two indexes may share one relation owner while using distinct index structures.
Conversely, changing relation storage can invalidate an index's row handles or
access assumptions, so these choices are checked together. $\Gamma$ records
the resulting physical values, field interpretation and current views; it does
not impose \texttt{\&Row} as the universal representation.
Compatibility is a domain obligation of each rule, with applicable type and
lifetime checks delegated to Rust; the generic compiler is not a semantic
theorem prover.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous queries.}
A block-backed relation, a hash-indexed relation and a trie-backed relation
must be able to retain distinct provider bindings in the same query.
Zone-map pruning motivates block metadata and payload access; future WCOJ
motivates trie traversal. These examples state an extensibility requirement,
not implemented operators or a claim that the current binary fragment already
expresses WCOJ. Each extension needs its own semantic and compilation rules.

The fixture in Section~\ref{sec:ix-physical-redesign} supplies a complete local
Physical payload with external provider and lowering paths. Its concrete rules
instantiate the boundary; they are not cases to add to the common compiler.
Provider callbacks are administrative realization steps. Retaining a semantic
provider operation as a dialect requires its own specification; no such extra
public IR is compulsory. A runtime RA evaluator is not required.

\paragraph{Conformance status.}
IX, structural selection and generic realization are implemented, with external
borrowed slice/hash/sorted providers and exact indexed joins. Actual macro
captures and independent runtime checks cover that admitted domain. General
stored intermediates and block/YA realization remain migration gaps. The old
global provider and whole-query routes have been removed. Authored local and
auxiliary fixtures are still distinguished from the executable complete-query
capture; fixture agreement is not proof of arbitrary external provider laws.
