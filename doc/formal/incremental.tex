\section{Incremental evaluation and Datalog (design)}
\label{sec:incremental}
This section specifies a proposed extension, not a verified implementation.
The rule inventory records implementation status separately. The design uses
sets and bags, insertions only, and totally ordered rounds. It does not
parameterize the existing compiler by a general semiring. Signed changes,
partially ordered time and recursive aggregates are outside this scope.

\subsection{One call: collections, iterators and partiality}
For notation, let $\mathrm{Rel}_K(X)=X\to_{fin}K$, with
$K\in\{\mathbb B,\mathbb N\}$. These are finite sets and finite bags,
respectively. Each has a monad $M_K$: unit assigns weight one to a value;
bind multiplies weights along a valuation and adds weights across valuations.
The coefficient map $n\mapsto(n>0)$ induces the support map
$\mathrm{supp}:M_{\mathbb N}X\to M_{\mathbb B}X$, which commutes with unit,
bind and union. Write $\iota$ for the characteristic-bag embedding of a set
and $\mathsf{canon}=\iota\circ\mathrm{supp}$. Canonicalization is idempotent;
$\iota$ itself is not a homomorphism for overlapping unions.

The written-order denotation remains exactly that of
\S\ref{sec:denotation}: $\bot+M_{\mathbb B}$, with strict propagation of a
panic from any reached valuation. A relational atom extends a valuation, a
guard retains or discards it, and the head projects or maps it.
Commutativity of the \emph{total} set/bag fragment does not establish
commutativity of arbitrary partial computations: an empty earlier relation
can prevent a later panic. Recognized comparisons and negated atoms still
require their binders and pure value-based behavior; given those, they move
like relational selections (CQ-GuardRecognize, CQ-Neg, C-Ready, E-Constraint,
E-Neg). Their definedness justifies motion (Motion lemma,
Section~\ref{sec:premises}); opaque guards keep the Rust chain.

An iterator supplies an ordered, demand-driven operational sequence, not a
bag by definition. For a finite successful exhaustive traversal, forgetting
order yields a bag and taking support yields a set. Push and pull are
possible realizations of that traversal; arbitrary Rust iterators are not
assumed to satisfy pure list-monad laws. Partial polling, effects, divergence
and resource exhaustion stay outside the observation model, as before.
Let $\mathsf{obs}$ take the support of a successful traversal and map an
observed panic to $\bot$.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{INC-Adequacy}{\text{host premises and checked lowering}\quad
 \text{exhaustive observation in the existing model}}
 {\sem{q}\sqsubseteq\mathsf{obs}(\mathsf{run}(q))}

\Rule{INC-Supp}{E_{\mathbb N},E_{\mathbb B}\text{ the same total positive context}}
 {\mathrm{supp}(E_{\mathbb N}(b))
   =E_{\mathbb B}(\mathrm{supp}(b))
   =\mathrm{supp}(E_{\mathbb N}(\mathsf{canon}(b)))}
\end{mathpar}
Adequacy is refinement, not equality of panics: successful source results
must agree; a target may remove a source panic. \textsc{INC-Supp} applies to
positive bind, projection, union and value-based filtering. Extensions to
partial code must separately discharge the existing extensionality and
Before obligations. This is not a blanket rewrite through negation,
first-nonempty fallback, aggregates or effectful consumers.

A consumer may own the final canonical boundary only if its contract states
that multiplicity is unobservable there. Canonicalizing $N$ removes repeated
rows \emph{inside} $N$; it does not remove rows already in another relation
$R$. The latter requires $\mathrm{supp}(N)\setminus R$.
For a pure fold of row values into a commutative idempotent monoid, duplicate
rows do not affect the answer. Count and sum generally require canonical
input. Counting a set is not a homomorphism for overlapping set union; no
such law is assumed for general aggregates.

\subsection{Representations and their obligations}
A full-row structure has an abstraction
$\alpha_S:S\to\mathrm{Rel}_K(X)$ and a lawful builder:
\[
 \alpha_S(\mathsf{build}_S(b))=b.
\]
A projected or filtered index instead builds its recorded view $V_L(b)$.
It need not retain enough information to reconstruct the source relation.
\begin{obligation}[per representation, as host premises]
A probe enumerates exactly the abstract rows matching the requested key,
with the representation's required multiplicities and payload reconstruction.
A merge satisfies
$\alpha_S(s\oplus s')=\alpha_S(s)+_K\alpha_S(s')$.
An optimized disjoint merge must satisfy the same equation under its stated
support-disjointness premise. Retyping preserves the observation contract,
not the concrete abstraction function or storage layout.
\end{obligation}
Source-row disjointness does not imply disjoint projected keys. For example,
$\{(a,1)\}$ and $\{(a,2)\}$ are disjoint but their projections onto the first
column overlap. A membership layout must still combine equal keys correctly.
Even a full-row hash or sorted index may share key prefixes across disjoint
inputs; a fast merge cannot simply assume disjoint buckets.
A canonical membership provider must decide full-tuple identity exactly;
covering all columns syntactically is not sufficient without that law.

\begin{definition}[Generic runs]
$\mathsf{Runs}\langle S\rangle$ holds contiguous intervals $[l_i,u_i)$,
each containing an immutable representation $r_i$. Its full abstraction is
$\sum_i\alpha_S(r_i)$. At an available boundary $u$, its exclusive cut is
\[
 \alpha(\mathsf{cut}_u(x))=\sum_{u_i\le u}\alpha_S(r_i).
\]
A cut inside a merged interval is unavailable unless the representation has
an additional, explicitly specified capability to recover it.
\end{definition}
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{STR-Maintain}{\text{lawful builds, merges and reads for }S\\
 \text{insert each interval exactly once in order}\\
 \text{merge adjacent runs without crossing a protected cut}}
 {\alpha(\mathsf{insert}(x,r))=\alpha(x)+_K\alpha_S(r)\\
  \alpha(\mathsf{merge}(x))=\alpha(x)\quad
  \alpha(\mathsf{cut}_u(\mathsf{merge}(x)))=\alpha(\mathsf{cut}_u(x))}
\end{mathpar}
\begin{proposition}[Parametric maintenance]
Starting from an empty entry, insert exact increments $\delta R_0,\ldots,
\delta R_t$, once each. Then its abstraction is
$\sum_{j=0}^t\delta R_j$. Every protected cut $u$ denotes
$\sum_{j<u}\delta R_j$. These claims follow by induction on insert and merge
operations, assuming the representation obligations.
\end{proposition}
The cut convention is exclusive: a version after inserting round $t$ uses
boundary $t+1$. Protection must precede a merge that would destroy a cut;
requesting an already destroyed cut is not sound. Persistent snapshots or
copy-on-write may discharge the same observation contract differently.
A query obtains a version-consistent view of all required indexes before it
runs. Publication of a new version and updates to all its indexes belong to
the shared maintenance protocol, not to individual index semantics.

The shared protocol owns rounds, publication, cut retention and scheduling;
concrete structures own layout and lawful build/merge/probe operations.
A selected backend may fuse operations but must refine that protocol.
These are semantic laws and capability requirements, not a claim that a
wrapper, merging, or multi-run probing has zero runtime cost.

\subsection{Changes without subtraction}
Option A uses supplied increments, not an invertible difference operator.
For a stream $a_t$ of increments define
\[
 A_{-1}=0,\qquad A_t=I_K(a)_t=\sum_{j=0}^t a_j.
\]
Boolean integration is union and loses redundant insertions, so it cannot
have a two-sided inverse. Natural-number subtraction is not defined for
arbitrary decreasing snapshots either. An insert-only contract instead
supplies $a_t$ and requires $A_t=A_{t-1}+_K a_t$.

For a total positive join, multilinearity gives the exact batch expansion
below; $A_{i,t}$ is the new accumulated input, and $a_{i,t}$ its supplied
increment. All unchanged inputs are held fixed.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CQ-Diff}{A_{i,t}=A_{i,t-1}+_K a_{i,t}\quad K\in\{\mathbb B,\mathbb N\}}
 {\prod_i A_{i,t}=\prod_i A_{i,t-1}+_K
   \sum_i\Big(\prod_{j<i}A_{j,t}\Big)a_{i,t}
                \Big(\prod_{j>i}A_{j,t-1}\Big)}
\end{mathpar}
Products denote joins, not multiplication of tuples. The expansion partitions
by the rightmost chosen increment and is exact for bags. When input sets
have fresh, disjoint increments it also partitions input valuations, but
projection or distinct rules can still produce the same output tuple.
Reading current versions at all non-delta positions instead is a covering
expansion: after support, union with the old result is still correct, though
it may enumerate a valuation repeatedly. Neither form removes the need to
reject already-known head tuples.

Let $N_t$ be the candidate bag emitted at round $t$, $R_{t-1}$ the committed
set before it, and $\delta R_t$ the accepted fresh set.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-InsertNew}{\delta R_t=\mathrm{supp}(N_t)\setminus R_{t-1}}
 {R_t=R_{t-1}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\delta R_t
     =R_{t-1}\cup\mathrm{supp}(N_t)\quad
   R_{t-1}\cap\delta R_t=\emptyset}
\end{mathpar}
A mutable staging set can implement both candidate self-deduplication and
membership against $R_{t-1}$, but readers of this round must continue to see
the old committed version. This may fuse physical operations; it does not
erase their distinct semantic obligations. Cost includes processing the
candidate bag, not only the accepted delta. A lawful maintained index needs
work proportional to its update/merge algorithm; no universal
$O(|\delta R_t|)$ round bound follows from these equations.

\paragraph{Maintained device runs.}
Let $V_L$ retain the complete row in the recorded permutation $L$.
Admission, rather than key uniqueness, supplies disjointness:
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-RunPartition}{\textsc{DL-InsertNew}\text{ at every round}\\
 \text{each admitted delta inserted exactly once}}
 {V_L(R^t)=\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j}\alpha(L_j)}

\Rule{STR-RunRead}{V_L(R)=\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{j}\alpha(L_j)}
 {\mathsf{lookup}(R,k)=\biguplus_j\mathsf{lookup}(L_j,k)\\
  \mathsf{member}(R,k)=\bigvee_j\mathsf{member}(L_j,k)}

\Rule{STR-RunCompact}{L'\text{ is the sorted merge of adjacent runs }L_a,\ldots,L_b}
 {\alpha(L')=\biguplus_{j=a}^{b}\alpha(L_j)}

\Rule{STR-RunSnapshot}{\text{publish all indexes of round }t\text{ before its readers}\\
 \text{enqueue all readers before insertion, compaction and retirement}}
 {\text{every round-}t\text{ read observes exactly }V_L(R^t)}
\end{mathpar}
The union in \textsc{STR-RunRead} is a bag union: equal key prefixes
may occur in several runs but complete rows remain disjoint. Compaction
preserves multiplicity, not merely support. A snapshot protects the exclusive
cut $t+1$; stream ordering retains its buffers until all enqueued readers
finish. No historical cut is promised after retirement. A leveled capacity
ratio is explicit maintenance advice (default four), not a semantic premise;
it cannot change admission, the multiset, or snapshot publication.
The device Storage constructor resolves \code{ratio(n)} (omission means four)
to a printable integer $\rho\ge2$. Every accepted/layout run state receives
that integer; temporary sort states use the same advice. The runtime checks
the bound and uses $\rho$, never a hidden constant, in the merge threshold.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-DeviceRatio}{\rho=\mathrm{Storage.device.ratio}\ge2\quad
 \rho\text{ passed to every device run constructor}}
 {\mathrm{mergeTrailing}(A,N)\text{ merges while }
  |A_{\mathrm{last}}|\le\mathrm{saturatingMul}(|N|,\rho)\quad
  \textsc{STR-RunCompact}\text{ preserves the represented rows}}
\end{mathpar}

\subsection{Datalog and the fixpoint boundary}
The external \texttt{features/datalog} workspace implements this boundary above
the unmodified compiler. Its five language crates record and check source
rules, dependency SCCs, delta relations, semi-naive variants, storage choices
and explicit round programs. Separate stratify, incremental, storage and fork
passes connect these languages through the stage protocol; a terminal printer
uses the shared Datalog printing library. Each language is independently checked
and can enter the pipeline at its consuming pass (Section~\ref{sec:datalog-languages}).

A program distinguishes fixed extensional hosts from intensional relations.
The dependency graph includes every rule-visible reference. Safety requires
head and negative variables to be bound by allowed positive clauses.
Negative dependencies must point to completed lower strata. Aggregates and
nested blocks are rejected; arbitrary Rust captures cannot hide reads of
evolving intensional state.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-Strata}{G\text{ contains all signed dependency edges}\\
 \text{aggregates are rejected; no negative edge lies inside an SCC of }G}
 {\text{evaluate dependency SCCs in order, freezing lower-stratum inputs}}
\end{mathpar}
For the following set theorem all rule expressions are total and pure.
Within one SCC, let $S$ be the union of rules with no positive reference to
that SCC, and let $F(R)$ be the union of the remaining recursive rules.
The reference transition is explicitly inflationary:
\[
 G(R)=R\cup S\cup F(R),\qquad R^0=\emptyset,\quad R^{t+1}=G(R^t).
\]
Monotonicity alone would not imply $R\subseteq F(R)$ for arbitrary $R$.
Finite fixed input domains and recursive heads that only copy positive-atom
columns make this ascending chain stabilize; lower-stratum seeds may add a
finite set of values. The implementation also permits arbitrary Rust
expressions and body \texttt{let}-bound values in recursive heads.
For such value-generating rules, termination is the caller's responsibility:
the ascending chain need not stabilize, evaluation may diverge, and a run
that does not reach the least fixpoint is outside the denotation.
The finite stabilization claim below is conditional on a finite reachable
domain (and total, pure rule expressions), not a source-language restriction.

\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-Delta}{q\text{ a total positive recursive rule}\\
 V_i\text{ preserves body order, head, and all fixed inputs}\\
 V_i\text{ reads position }i\text{ from }\Delta
 \text{ and every other SCC position from }R\cup\Delta\\
 \{V_i\}\text{ covers exactly the positive SCC positions}}
 {q(R\cup\Delta)=q(R)\cup\textstyle\bigcup_i V_i(R\cup\Delta,\Delta)}
\end{mathpar}
Delta erasure is necessary but not sufficient: a checker must also validate
coverage, exact substitutions and dependencies. The executable Datalog boundary
admits only this semi-naive covering scheme, not an exact old/new partition.

Initialize $R^1=\Delta^1=S$. For $t\ge1$, evaluate the recursive variants
against immutable $R^t,\Delta^t$, then set
\[
 N^t=\biguplus_{q,i}V_{q,i}(R^t,\Delta^t),\qquad
 \Delta^{t+1}=\mathrm{supp}(N^t)\setminus R^t,\qquad
 R^{t+1}=R^t\mathbin{\dot\cup}\Delta^{t+1}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Total finite semi-naive evaluation]
The semi-naive and reference sequences agree at each round, and their
stabilized value is the least fixpoint of $G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Recursive rules have a positive SCC atom, so $F(\emptyset)=\emptyset$ and the
seed round agrees. Inductively,
$R^t=R^{t-1}\cup\Delta^t$ and $F(R^{t-1})\subseteq R^t$.
\textsc{DL-Delta} covers all newly enabled valuations; old derivations are
already present. \textsc{DL-InsertNew} removes only known head tuples.
Thus the next union is $R^t\cup S\cup F(R^t)$, as required.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Owned indexes, not a core incremental protocol.}
The Datalog layer owns one canonical all-column set for each derived relation.
After a round's CQ holes finish, successful \texttt{insert\_new} operations
produce precisely the next delta, and those fresh tuples update every selected
layout. Extensional layouts are built once per program. Each layout records a
complete column permutation and a leading key prefix, chosen from join
variables under delta-driven evaluation. Borrowed grouped map hosts expose
their existing indexes through the public \texttt{Rows}/\texttt{IndexRead}
interfaces. No compiler staging or maintained-storage extension is required.
The caller's CQ pipeline is unchanged: if its chosen probe does not match a
recorded layout, ordinary CQ fallback construction remains correct but may
cost more work. Layout choice is therefore a checked Datalog decision, not a
claim that every possible caller pipeline reuses every index.

Datalog emits a CQ negated atom against a key set built once at the consuming
stratum boundary, after its negative inputs are complete. E-Neg lowers that
atom to the existing RA anti-join; an exact host membership read uses the
borrowed key set without rebuilding it per call. Wildcards are omitted from
the key. Negated atoms introduce no bindings and do not turn a negative
dependency into a positive recursive edge.

\paragraph{Partial Rust is a separate obligation.}
Final-valuation coverage does not prove equality of panics: a guard may
panic on a prefix that a later empty atom prevents from producing any answer.
For partial code the required claim remains successful-source refinement,
with written chains and Before facts preserved for each generated call.
A multi-round proof must establish that every reached guard prefix is safe
whenever the reference evaluation succeeds. Neither the total fixpoint proof
nor a delta-renaming check alone supplies that argument. Interior mutation
through shared references is also not ruled out by borrowing; the existing
extensionality premise is still needed.
The executable Source, Incremental and Round references described in
Section~\ref{sec:datalog-languages} retain native Rust evaluation of these
written-order prefixes. The independent CQ integer-fragment evaluator cannot
interpret arbitrary opaque Rust or substitute for this panic-sensitive reference.
Successful-reference refinement checks compare output sets and reject new target
panics on each exercised instance; they do not prove the multi-round obligation
for all inputs or all partial Rust expressions.

\subsection{Ownership, comparison and remaining obligations}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{.22\linewidth}p{.68\linewidth}}
\toprule
Owner & Responsibility\\
\midrule
Datalog & Dependency strata, conditional finite-domain stabilization,
inflationary fixpoint, and candidate-to-fresh-set boundary.\\
CQ/RA & Checked per-call plans; explicit incremental expansion and version
requirements where that optional path is introduced.\\
Physical & Consumer-sensitive canonicalization and access scheduling.\\
Structures & Representation and maintenance choices; declared readers and
cuts, checked against a shared version protocol.\\
Runtime protocol & Publication, cut retention and generic run management.\\
Concrete type & Lawful build, merge, probe and any specialized capabilities.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Ordinary CQ compilation retains its snapshot denotation. An incremental
path must introduce an explicit state-transition contract; replacing an
opaque storage type alone cannot add fixpoint or temporal semantics.
For the Datalog implementation, the variant scheme is fixed to semi-naive
covering. Recorded choices include dedup-before-membership versus
membership-before-dedup and canonical layout. Broader incremental storage
designs may choose eager versus deferred merges and local versus shared indexes;
these are not additional Datalog evaluation schemes. Sharing must coordinate
each reader's version requirements. Merge policy cannot invalidate an outstanding cut.

Differential Dataflow provides a relevant implementation comparison at the
inspected revision~\cite{code-dd}. Its \code{TraceReader} specifies accumulated
updates and logical/physical compaction frontiers; \code{Spine<B>} and
\code{CursorList<C>} already share substantial maintenance and reading logic.
\code{OrdValMerger} and \code{OrdKeyMerger} each advance update timestamps;
the generic chunk interface likewise requires an advancement operation.
These are evidence of a particular boundary between shared contracts and
specialized implementations, not evidence that all temporal semantics is
duplicated or that unordered representations are semantically impossible.
Its \code{formal/Differential/Compositional.lean} models compositional
accumulation adequacy, but this is not a proof of our generated Rust or of
every DD storage implementation.

\label{sec:incremental-scope}
Proof obligations remain: representation laws for user types, partial-Rust
refinement across rounds, safe dedup handover, version publication and cut
liveness, and composition of actual generated code with the total model.
Rust trait bounds establish interfaces, not these laws. Property tests and
counterexamples supplement, but do not replace, those arguments.
Extending to signed weights or partially ordered time is a separate design:
it requires stateful support/threshold handling, progress and compaction
contracts, and appropriate operator laws. It is not just an annotation
parameter added to otherwise unchanged structures.
