\section{Representation and correctness of physical realization}
\label{sec:physical}
Section~\ref{sec:ix-physical-redesign} defines selection into Physical and
realization of that bound program.
This section states its representation and ownership obligations; it introduces
no alternative index analysis or post-IX route.

\paragraph{Selected program.}
Selection produces $P=(IX,\Phi,\Lambda)$ and preserves denotation by erasure
to IX. Realization must establish the representation claims of this particular
program. Every RA definition, including structural definitions, has its selected
rule in $\Lambda$; free host inputs are supplied by their relation bindings.
The realizing pass receives no policy. A selected rule that does not accept
the supplied representations causes a compilation failure, not a second choice.

\paragraph{Representation.}
For an exact RA version $r$, let $R=\sem r_{RA}$. The environment $\Gamma$
records a physical representation and its decoding relation to $R$.
Its carriers may be row references, tuples of references, or stable handles;
they need not be references to separately stored complete rows. A current view
denotes exactly its selected rows even when it shares immutable storage with
an earlier version. The selected instances $\Phi_R(r)$ and $\Phi_I(i)$ must
agree on that version and on their shared owners.

For $D(i)=(r,C)$, write $I_i\simeq_{r,C}R$ when
\[
 \forall k,\quad
 \set\{\operatorname{decode}_i(k,w)\mid
       w\in\operatorname{lookup}_{I_i}(\rho_i(k))\}
 =\{t\in R\mid\pi_C(t)=k\}.
\]
Here $k$ is a logical tuple of indexed-column values and $\rho_i$ gives its
chosen physical representation. Decoding may use both the lookup tuple and
the returned witness. For a relation stored as a map from $a$ to a vector of
$b$ values, a witness is a borrowed $b$ and decoding gives $(a,b)$.
The descriptor refers to the exact source version. Reusing an index built over
an earlier, unreduced version does not satisfy this relation in general.
Neither index identity nor the access-use association is inferred again here:
physical lowering reads the written $D$ and $U$.

\paragraph{Preparation and reuse.}
Physical preparation follows the provider instances already written in $\Phi$,
and each RA definition dispatches through its written $\Lambda$ entry. In the primary
fixture the input maps already realize the required indexes, so preparation
only binds their existing views. It need not allocate or scan the relation.
For an auxiliary map built over rows, a separate construction proof maintains
that each bucket contains precisely the matching carriers from the processed
prefix. The empty map and one-carrier insertion establish this invariant;
source exhaustion establishes the exact-index law. Equality and hashing, or
ordering, must agree with logical equality. These are example provider proofs,
not compiler-core cases that enumerate every storage family.

\paragraph{Lifetimes and placement.}
Each required index is bound to a valid realization after its exact source
version becomes available and before its first use. An existing index can be
reused; a constructed index is prepared once for that binding. Several indexes
may share one owner, while remaining distinct IX definitions. No provider may
invalidate another instance's handles by silently replacing shared storage.
Source and index owners must outlive the references that escape their uses.
A tuple of borrowed fields does not require an owned tuple of payload values.
Unmet representation, trait, or lifetime premises reject compilation; hidden
payload clones or unrequested conversions are not a repair rule.

\begin{lemma}[Indexed join and semijoin]
Suppose $I_i\simeq_{r,C}\sem r_{RA}$, $U(j.\mathsf{right})=i$, and $C$
is the written IX descriptor for $j$. An exact indexed realization that
enumerates matching witnesses for join, or tests their existence for semijoin,
preserves the corresponding set denotation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a left row $x$, the lookup law gives exactly the right rows agreeing with
$x$ on the written join columns. Merging each such row therefore gives exactly
its natural-join witnesses. Nonemptiness gives precisely the semijoin predicate.
Taking output support removes repeated occurrences without changing either set.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Host expressions}
\label{sec:host-elab}
The representation in $\Gamma$ translates logical columns to concrete access
expressions over the selected carrier.
Rust expressions embedded in an Access clause retain their lexical environment.
Reading a borrowed field does not imply ownership of its payload. Producing a
new owned output is a separate, explicit representation decision.
\begin{obligation}[Host adequacy]
For every admitted host expression and represented binding, elaboration resolves
the intended logical names, preserves the declared value, and satisfies the
chosen Rust type and ownership requirements. Parsing, free-variable analysis,
and successful macro expansion alone do not establish this obligation.
\end{obligation}

The current compiler does not yet implement the complete $(A,D,U)$ contract
or the separate Physical selection and realization boundaries.
These conditional results specify what a replacement must establish; they are
not a correctness claim for the existing post-IX implementation.
