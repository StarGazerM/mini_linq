\section{Scope and structure}
\label{sec:introduction}
A conjunctive query specifies satisfying valuations, not containers, indexes
or access algorithms. MiniLinq plans it, records required index demands, and
lets caller-selected passes choose physical operators and concrete Rust
representations. The compiled chain is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\mathsf{CQ}\xrightarrow{\text{normalize}}\mathsf{CQ_{NF}}
 \xrightarrow{\text{order}}\mathsf{VO}\xrightarrow{\text{reduce}}\mathsf{VO}
 \xrightarrow{\text{elaborate}}\mathsf{RA'}\\
 &\quad\xrightarrow{\text{index}}\mathsf{IX}
 \xrightarrow{\text{lower}}\mathsf{Physical}
 \xrightarrow{\text{select}}\mathsf{Structures}
 \xrightarrow{\text{print}}\mathsf{Rust}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every intermediate program is complete: it owns its declarations and output
and can be parsed, checked and continued without its producer. VO extends CQ,
IX extends RA prime and Structures extends Physical; RA prime and Physical
stand alone. Section~\ref{sec:denotation} fixes the denotation;
Sections~\ref{sec:cq}--\ref{sec:structures} give one section per language;
Section~\ref{sec:passes} gives one subsection per pass;
Section~\ref{sec:composition} gives the pass protocol and
Section~\ref{sec:validation} the evidence.

\paragraph{Pass obligation.} For a pass $f:L\to M$ with explicit arguments
$\chi$ and observation relation $\alpha$,
\[
 \mathsf{WF}_L(s)\land\mathsf{Domain}_f(s,\chi)
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 f(s,\chi)=t\land\mathsf{WF}_M(t)\land\sem s_L\sqsubseteq\alpha(\sem t_M).
\]
An unmet domain premise is diagnosed. A pass may not invent a missing demand
or substitute another source version. Each pass has a separately callable
$\mathsf{check}_f(s,t,\chi)$ that never reruns $f$.

\paragraph{Out of scope.} Negation, aggregation, correlated subqueries and
recursion are external surface consumers composed from checked core
invocations, not core operators. General cost-based physical search,
cardinality estimation and a worst-case-optimal-join complexity guarantee are
not implemented. Rust resolution, ownership and the runtime trait laws for
arbitrary user types are proof obligations supported by tests, not theorems
of this paper.
