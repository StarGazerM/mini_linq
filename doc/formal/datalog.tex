\section{Datalog language boundaries and contracts}
\label{sec:datalog-languages}
This specification separates five checked languages above the unchanged CQ
compiler. Structural checks do not discharge all semantic obligations.
Contracts admit recorded choices; default producers choose deterministically.
The base admits dependency-ordered unions of SCCs, alternative usable layouts
and opaque concrete Rust providers, while retaining the
\code{seminaive\_covering} evaluation scheme and a checked operational vocabulary.
Structural checks examine the supplied IR and never rerun a producer to compare
with its chosen output.
Declaration pruning removes unused hole-local declarations and bindings.
The Fx-default cutover changes hash-provider type paths. The context-discharge
cutover assigns each hole's returned set boundary to its checked consumer and
derives input uniqueness from structural storage certificates, eliminating
the default hole-local deduplication rewrite. It does not change the
set-valued source meaning or the core CQ compiler.

\paragraph{Semantic domain and limits.}
Each relation declares a model slot $M$; the base accepts only \code{set}.
EDB hosts denote sets even when their physical traversal contains duplicates.
Outputs denote sets, not their iteration order. Rust expressions are opaque,
pure partial functions, with panic represented by $\bot$; purity and lawful
provider operations are premises, not syntactic checks. A terminating,
successful source denotes the stratified least fixpoint. Divergence has no
completed result. Computed heads and finite generators do not by themselves
guarantee a finite reachable domain. Refinement requires that a successful
reference yield the same sets without a new panic; it does not require equal
panic domains. Observers and their evaluation counts are outside this relational
meaning. A different model requires a dialect meaning and a checked translation;
no pass may silently change $M$.

\subsection{DL-Surface}
\paragraph{Grammar.} Raw tokens containing source syntax and supported sugar.
\paragraph{Contract.} Surface passes check their own expansion; the generic
raw-token language has no Datalog well-formedness judgment.
\paragraph{Meaning.} Meaning is that of the translated DL-Source program.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} None; surface syntax is eliminated before
source parsing. In particular, parsing a later IR must not conditionally
expand \code{if let}.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Preserve unowned syntax and validate the owned
expansion, including its binders and evaluation position.

\subsection{DL-Source}
\paragraph{Grammar.} Declarations (EDB bindings or IDB declarations, each with
model \code{set}), ordered rules, outputs, and an optional observer. Bodies
contain positive atoms, safe negative atoms, and written-order CQ Rust steps
including \code{let}, \code{for}, and guards; heads may be computed.
\paragraph{Contract.} Declared names, arities, types and outputs are consistent;
head and negative variables are safely bound; negative variables must occur
in an earlier positive atom, not only a scalar \code{let} or generator.
Signed dependencies are stratifiable. Aggregates and nested blocks remain
outside this base language.
Opaque Rust must not hide reads of evolving IDBs.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Source}{\text{source safety, declarations and signed stratifiability}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Source}}(P)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Evaluate components in dependency order, taking the
inflationary least fixpoint of their immediate consequences, with lower
components fixed. The executable \code{eval\_source} reference implements this
synchronous transition with written-order native Rust rule evaluation, independently
of production stratification and incrementalization.
The total finite theorem in the incremental section applies under its premises;
partial execution additionally has the obligations below.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Relation model, EDB bindings, written rule and
Rust-step order, outputs and observer. No schedule or physical choices.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both Source contracts and preserve the declared
interface: program name, relation declarations and models, EDB bindings, outputs
and observer.
Rules may change, including opaque Rust payloads. Preservation of relational
meaning and partial-execution refinement is a semantic premise of such a rewrite,
validated by executable refinement cases, not decided by the structural gate.

\subsection{DL-Strata}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Source plus an ordered list of components.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Source, then require a complete, nonoverlapping
partition of the IDBs into nonempty components, each a union of whole dependency
SCCs. A positive IDB dependency is in the same or an earlier component; a negative
IDB dependency is strictly earlier. Thus no component contains internal negation.
EDBs remain fixed inputs. Independent components may be reordered and positive
SCCs may be merged when these conditions hold.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Strata}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Source}}(P)\quad
 C\text{ completely partitions SCCs and respects signed dependency order}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(P,C)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Sequential composition of component fixpoints, equal to
the source meaning under the stratification premises.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Component membership and order.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Source exactly;
the target may choose a different admissible component partition or order.
The SCC theorem in the incremental section extends to each such union: its internal dependencies
are positive, and all negative inputs are frozen.

\subsection{DL-Incremental}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Strata plus fresh delta declarations and ordered
seed/step variants, each identifying its source rule and delta occurrence.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Strata and accept only the recorded scheme
\code{seminaive\_covering}. Check fresh delta names and matching types, rule
identity, variant erasure and changing-position coverage directly. Each rule
with no positive occurrence in its current component has at least one seed;
otherwise each changing positive occurrence is selected by at least one step
variant reading its delta, while other changing occurrences read the accumulated
relation. Preserve head, written body order, Rust steps and fixed inputs under
erasure. Repeated seed and step variants are allowed: covering is not a disjoint
or exactly-once derivation partition. Variant order and fresh names need not equal
the default producer's choices.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Incremental}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(S)\quad
 V\text{ has valid deltas, erasure and seminaive covering coverage}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(S,V)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Starting from the deduplicated seeds, each round reads
immutable accumulated $A_r$ and delta $\Delta_r$, computes candidates $C_r$,
and admits and publishes
\[
 \Delta_{r+1}=\mathrm{supp}(C_r)\setminus A_r,\qquad
 A_{r+1}=A_r\cup\Delta_{r+1}.
\]
Stop when all component deltas are empty. These are set operations; the model
slot does not authorize lattice subtraction or semiring differences.
The executable \code{eval\_incremental} reference consumes the supplied checked
variants rather than regenerating a default schedule. It evaluates seeds, then
the recorded covering steps against immutable accumulated and delta sets,
deduplicating and admitting candidates at the round boundary.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Covering semi-naive scheme, delta names,
variant identities and occurrence substitutions. Naive and disjoint exact
schemes are not accepted merely because they are conceivable alternatives.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve the underlying
Strata program exactly; delta declarations and variant schedules may change
provided the target independently satisfies erasure and coverage.

\paragraph{Frozen negation.}
Let $B$ contain completed lower-component relations. Within the consuming
component, a negative literal is a fixed predicate of the currently bound
values, not a changing positive position. It is neither delta-substituted nor
counted toward coverage. For total pure rules, the usual covering argument
therefore applies with $B$ held fixed:
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-FrozenNegation}{B\text{ is complete and immutable}\quad
 q_B\text{ is total and monotone in its positive current-component inputs}}
 {q_B(R\cup\Delta)=q_B(R)\cup\bigcup_i V_{B,i}(R\cup\Delta,\Delta)}
\end{mathpar}
Wildcards are existentially projected out of negative membership keys.
Key construction applies constant filters and repeated-variable equalities,
then projects one column per distinct variable, in first-occurrence order.
These completed sets are built once per stratum; each emitted \code{!K(args)}
has distinct named arguments, or one wildcard for the unit-key emptiness case.
No rule above permits negation of a changing current-component relation.

\paragraph{Partial Rust across rounds.}
Erasure, body-order preservation and full-valuation coverage do not prove
prefix safety: a partial expression can run on a prefix whose later atom is
empty. The additional obligation is that every prefix evaluated by every
generated variant is safe whenever the written-order reference succeeds,
including seed execution and cross-round repetition. Per-hole CQ checks and
Before facts constrain each call but do not discharge this multi-round
obligation. The total finite fixpoint theorem is not claimed for arbitrary
partial Rust. Executable panic/refinement cases provide evidence, not a proof
for all opaque expressions.

\subsection{DL-Storage}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Incremental plus a declaration-ordered
\code{providers} vector, full and delta layouts, occurrence-to-read bindings,
frozen negative projections, and insertion/negation policies. Each
\code{Providers} record contains a local \code{canonical\_name}, the opaque Rust
type \code{canonical\_set} with generic row parameter \code{R}, and concrete
row-container types \code{all}, \code{seen}, \code{delta} and \code{next}.
The opaque expressions \code{all\_init}, \code{delta\_init} and
\code{next\_init} initialize their respective containers. Each \code{Layout}
records its relation, complete column permutation, key-prefix length,
opaque concrete map \code{provider} type and \code{maintenance} operation.
A \code{Read} records the variant, written body position, read kind, structure
index and column order. A \code{Negative} records its consuming component,
source relation, distinct-variable columns, constant filters and positional
repeated-variable equalities.

\paragraph{Textual records.} Storage and Round use the existing
\code{record!} grammar, not a second surface language: fields occur in their
declared order as \code{field \{ payload \}}; a vector is a bracketed sequence
of braced entries without commas; an option is \code{[]} or
\code{[payload]}. Thus a two-member index vector is \code{[\{0\}\{1\}]}.
Rust types and expressions are parsed losslessly as opaque Rust syntax inside
their field delimiters. Parse/print roundtripping retains these choices;
resuming a checked IR does not consult a producer to recover them.

\paragraph{Contract.} Check Incremental, require one provider record per source
declaration, and accept the supported all-column insertion and borrowed
negation policies \code{all\_columns} and
\code{borrowed}. Every full layout records
\code{admitted\_rows} maintenance; every delta layout records
\code{replace\_each\_run}. Each declared layout has a complete column
permutation and valid key prefix, including additional unused layouts.
For every recorded read, locate the actual source-rule occurrence and derive
the variables already bound at that written position and its constants.
The selected structure must belong to the correct relation and version, and
its key must use only available columns; a recorded bound-column claim is not
evidence. Require exactly one binding per positive occurrence and no extraneous
bindings, together with the independently derived frozen negative projection,
constant filters and repeated-variable equalities, including wildcard treatment.
A \code{direct} binding reads the selected
delta relation in declaration column order; it need not be the first body
occurrence. Full and delta layouts must serve their respective versions.
The checker does not whitelist applicable Rust provider types or interpret their
initializer expressions; typing and operation availability are checked by Rust.
It does require syntactically identical \code{delta} and \code{next} types,
because \code{delta\_swap} moves the entire next container into delta.
The uniform provider record reserves fields for both relation kinds. For an
EDB, the unused \code{all}, \code{seen}, \code{delta}, \code{next} and their
initializers must retain the default row-container records. For an IDB,
the unused \code{canonical\_name} and \code{canonical\_set} must retain their
defaults. Changes to these inapplicable fields are rejected, not silently ignored.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Storage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(I)\quad
 D\text{ records complete providers, usable reads and maintained layouts}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(I,D)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning and provider premises.} Data refinement by $\alpha$ requires
the EDB canonical provider and each published full layout to represent the
same relation, delta layouts to represent precisely the current fresh delta,
and negative keys to represent the prescribed projection/filter of frozen
inputs. Initializers and default construction must denote empty containers.
In particular, membership sets, initial full/delta layout hosts, and every
delta-layout reset use \code{::core::default::Default::default()}.
Default construction is part of these operations' meaning, not an independent
initializer choice: each selected provider must implement \code{Default} as
the empty represented collection, including every empty delta-run rebuild.
Canonical collection removes duplicates without changing row values; canonical
iteration and cloning preserve those values. The membership provider's
\code{insert} returns true exactly for a previously absent complete row,
and retains that row. Sequence push, extension, traversal and owned iteration
preserve their rows; moving the next container to delta preserves exactly the
admitted batch. Map entry lookup and bucket insertion implement the recorded
key/payload split, without losing rows or mixing unequal keys. Equality,
hashing, ordering (where required), cloning and borrowing obey the relevant
provider laws, and these operations introduce no new panic on successful
reference executions. These are semantic premises, not proofs obtained by
parsing a type name or by checking method availability.

\paragraph{Canonical host certificates.}
The \code{certificates} vector records typed \code{HostCanonical} entries:
each has a \code{kind}, structure \code{index}, structural \code{source}
and \code{columns} provenance. Admitted kind/source pairs are
\code{all/provider} for canonical EDBs, \code{all/admission} and
\code{delta/admission} for IDBs, \code{layout/all} and
\code{delta\_layout/delta} for maintained layouts, and
\code{negation/projection} for frozen projected keys.
A layout certificate requires its exact complete column permutation,
maintenance operation and certified all/delta root. Certificates may cover
only a subset of the storage hosts; an uncertified host receives no discharged
fact. Eligibility follows structural provenance, not a provider-type whitelist.
Opaque lawful collection and maintenance remain premises, not properties
proved by inspecting Rust type names.
Canonical EDB collection establishes distinct complete rows even when the
original EDB traversal repeats them. IDB \code{all} starts empty and
receives only admitted fresh batches; \code{delta} is precisely one such batch.
A full layout receives each admitted row once, and a delta layout is reset and
rebuilt from the current batch. Their complete column permutations preserve
full-row identity, including the key/payload split. Thus buckets may contain
many payloads with the same key, but not repeated complete represented rows.
Frozen-key collection separately establishes a set of the recorded projected
keys; projection alone would not establish uniqueness.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-HostCanonical}{
 \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(D)\quad
 \mathsf{certified}_D(H)\quad
 \mathsf{provenance}(H)\in
 \{\mathsf{canonicalEDB},\mathsf{admittedAll},\mathsf{admittedDelta},
   \mathsf{maintainedLayout},\mathsf{collectedKeys}\}\quad
 \mathsf{operations}(H)\text{ satisfy the corresponding provider laws}}
 {\mathsf{fullRows}(H)\text{ has no duplicates}}
\end{mathpar}
The Storage checker establishes the relation/version, permutation and
maintenance premises from supplied records. Round additionally checks copied
storage, publication coverage and order, and frozen-key construction before
using a certificate at a hole. Neither checker reruns storage selection or
Fork, and neither proves the laws of an opaque provider from its Rust type.
A complete immutable scan or an independently checked slice of such a host
inherits uniqueness; a raw external binding does not.

\paragraph{Defaults and checked alternatives.} The default canonical wrapper
is named \code{Canonical}, with \code{::std::collections::HashSet<R, Fx>} storage,
where \code{Fx} abbreviates the recorded concrete type
\code{::std::hash::BuildHasherDefault<::cq\_datalog::rustc\_hash::FxHasher>}.
The accumulated, delta and next providers are concrete \code{Vec} row types,
initialized with \code{::std::vec::Vec::new()}; membership uses
\code{::std::collections::HashSet<Tuple, Fx>} of complete rows. Layouts use
\code{::std::collections::HashMap} from key tuples to \code{Vec} payload
tuples, with the same Fx build hasher. These are default records, not
the definition of admissibility. A concrete \code{BTreeMap} layout with the
same key/payload types is admitted under its lawful ordering premise.
Likewise a later changing occurrence may select a direct delta scan instead
of a delta layout, retaining its written position and declaration-order columns.

\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Concrete provider types, wrapper name,
container initializers, layout shape and maintenance, occurrence bindings,
negative projections and the supported insertion/negation operations.
Applicable opaque provider syntax is retained through Fork and consumed by the
printer, rather than replaced with default types; inapplicable fields are
checked reserved defaults.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Incremental exactly;
allow changed provider records, layouts and occurrence bindings when the target
satisfies coverage, key availability, version and maintenance obligations.
Provider refinement remains a semantic premise of such a rewrite.

\subsection{DL-Round}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Storage plus a \code{RoundContext} of runtime
relations, copied concrete providers and layouts, component \code{Stratum}
records, outputs and observer bindings; complete embedded CQ holes; and a
CQ sub-pipeline and typed interface for each hole. A \code{Stratum} records
members, component-local negative keys, ordered seed/step \code{RuleHole}
lists, explicit phases, publication operations, termination references and test,
observer placement, key scope and loop form. Each \code{RuleHole} records the variant, head,
hole index, optional scan binding and membership \code{insertion} operation.
Each runtime \code{Relation} records \code{admit}, a vector of owned rule-hole
indices. Each \code{Hole} records \code{facts: cq\_stage::Context}, with a
consumer-canonical flag and a set of discharged host names.
Its \code{lower} field records the exact lowering invocation ordinal in the
CQ queue, rather than identifying that boundary only by a path spelling.
An \code{Output} records a relation reference and conversion operation.
The program also records a typed \code{terminals} registry mapping exact macro
paths to \code{pull} or \code{push}; duplicate paths are rejected.
\code{HoleInterface::Pull} denotes an iterator of result rows.
\code{HoleInterface::Push} denotes a terminal that invokes a supplied consumer
once for each result row, without an intermediate result collection.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Storage, exact copied provider/layout correspondence,
hole correspondence under the recorded reads, seed/step ordering, fresh runtime
names and the operational obligations below. The check does not reconstruct a
default storage or Round plan. Each hole's recorded interface must equal the
interface registered for its sub-pipeline's terminal path. An absent terminal,
an unregistered path or a mismatched interface is rejected. Terminal arguments
belong to that terminal's own checked argument grammar, not an argument-free
allowlist. Leading path qualification is significant.
For example, \code{terminals = [crate::alias::pull => pull];} in
\code{DatalogConfiguration}, together with
\code{cq = pipeline \{ print = crate::alias::pull; \};}, explicitly replaces
the registry. Fork serializes the complete typed registry into Round text and
records the selected terminal's interface in each hole, so a resumed Round
needs neither the originating configuration nor CQ compilation to check it.
Each CQ hole declares the relations, deltas and layouts it reads positively,
its borrowed completed key-set hosts, any scan binding, and its output head.
Negation is a CQ \code{!K(args)} atom, not a Rust predicate capture.
The Round checker independently derives the required declarations, host
bindings, distinct-variable projections and repeated-variable equalities.
Declaring an alternate path asserts its calling convention and semantic printer
obligation; it is not Rust alias resolution or a proof of printer equivalence.
The argument-free \code{own\_push::push!} is not a push-interface terminal:
although it uses push loops internally, its public result is an iterator.
Datalog's push adapter uses the public consumer form of that feature printer.
This check examines terminal invocations, not Rust tokens that do not yet exist.
With $\Gamma$ the recorded registry and $\mathrm{last}(Q)$ the terminal:
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Hole}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{CQ}}(h)\quad
 \mathrm{last}(Q)=(p,a)\quad \Gamma(p)=\iota}
 {\Gamma\vdash(h,Q,\iota)\ \mathrm{hole}}
\end{mathpar}
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Round}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(D)\quad
 H\text{ implements the recorded variants and frozen keys}\quad
 E\text{ has complete ordered maintenance, termination and outputs}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Round}}(D,H,E)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} A state machine over storage: evaluations see the
committed snapshot; admission rejects known and duplicate candidate heads;
publication extends full structures and replaces every delta layout, including
empty ones, before subsequent evaluation. Under provider and partial-Rust
premises its $\alpha$-image refines the Incremental transition system.
The executable \code{eval\_round} reference consumes the supplied checked round
program and evaluates its holes with native Rust, interpreting the recorded
execution over a logical storage state rather than calling the production printer.
This makes its result an independent comparison point for round rewrites and
generated execution; it does not establish the laws of opaque concrete providers.
Pull and push differ only at the CQ boundary: a pull iterator feeds the
admission loop, while a push terminal receives that same admission operation
as its consumer. Frozen-key holes similarly feed the recorded key provider.
Neither interface permits publication during evaluation.

\paragraph{Admission owners and per-hole context.}
Each runtime relation's \code{admit} list records its admission ownership.
Across all component seed/step rules, every rule-hole index appears exactly
once, in its declared head relation's list, using all-column \code{insert};
no owner may name a missing, foreign, duplicate or wrong-head hole. This
ownership is checked independently against the supplied rule-hole records,
rather than inferred from a producer's usual ordering. Frozen-key construction
holes instead belong to exactly one recorded \code{KeySet.hole} consumer.
Neither case requires the returned candidate stream
itself to be duplicate-free.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-Admit-Owner}{
 \mathsf{owners}(h)=\{R\}\quad \mathsf{head}(h)=R\quad
 \mathsf{admit}_R\text{ uses complete-row membership insertion}}
 {\mathsf{admit}_R(B)=\mathsf{admit}_R(\mathrm{supp}(B))}
\end{mathpar}
Equality here concerns the admitted set and subsequent relational state, not
candidate traversal order, insertion-attempt counts or timing.
Under \textsc{DL-Admit-Owner}, a rule hole records
\code{consumer canonical}; a frozen-key hole records the same fact under
the key provider's collection/insertion law. For every positively read host
$H$ with a valid structural certificate, \textsc{DL-HostCanonical} justifies
\code{host\_set H discharged}; uncertified hosts receive no such fact.
Facts refer to the hole's actual positive declarations after any checked scan
substitution, not to unused relation names. A negative key-set declaration is
canonical but is not a positive host-set argument for these facts.

These typed per-hole facts are part of the checked Round interface.
Raw CQ entry remains fact-free. Fork records the facts in the selected
\code{lower} argument packet, preserving the rest of the authored queue.
An explicit \code{fork lower=<usize>} argument selects a custom lowering
alias; otherwise the queue must contain a unique path with suffix
\code{lower}. Round independently parses the invocation at
\code{Hole.lower} as \code{cq\_stage::Context} and requires exact equality
with \code{Hole.facts}. Execution erasure renames both the discharged-host
facts and that packet; checks before erasure reject facts naming non-read
inputs. The core protocol also supports introduction through \code{select}.
Once the facts are recorded in Physical, every factful reentry carries
the exact explicit \code{context} envelope of Section~\ref{sec:context}:
\begin{lstlisting}
context { consumer = canonical, host_set H discharged };
pipeline { ... };
Program
\end{lstlisting}
The envelope equals the recorded consumer and discharged-host facts in both
directions; neither an omitted fact nor an additional authorization is allowed.
A matching lower/select packet does not authorize reentry to an already
factful source. Ordinary continuation derives its next envelope from the
next source rather than blindly forwarding the previous envelope.
The provenance check complements, rather than replaces, Round's proof of the
consumer and host facts.

\paragraph{Redundant return deduplication.}
Rule holes retain their projected heads. The consumer fact removes the
hole-return \code{\_\_cq\_seen} set and unnecessary \code{return canon};
there is no need to widen the head to all body variables. Discharged host facts
also seed CQ's uniqueness analysis without runtime host certification.
Internal canonicalization required by intermediate operators is unchanged.
Pull iterators, direct push consumers, and phase-timed candidate buffers all
end at the same checked canonical consumer; buffering does not transfer
ownership of the set boundary.

\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{P-DL-Push}{\Gamma\vdash(h,Q,\mathrm{push})\ \mathrm{hole}\quad
 c=\mathrm{admit}_{M}\ \text{or the recorded frozen-key insertion}}
 {\mathrm{execute}(h,Q,c)=Q(h;\mathrm{consumer}=c)}
\end{mathpar}

\paragraph{Frozen-key providers.} Each \code{KeySet} records its source relation,
distinct-variable columns, constant filters, repeated-variable equalities,
runtime name, construction hole and opaque Rust \code{provider} type. The default is
\code{::std::collections::HashSet<\_, Fx>}, with the same unkeyed Fx build
hasher as Storage; this is not a type whitelist.
Collection and borrowed membership must implement precisely the recorded
selection/projection set, with lawful equality and any required hashing or ordering.
The pull realization uses \code{FromIterator}; the push realization initializes
the provider with \code{Default} and extends it with each singleton row.
These operations must denote the same set.
Type correctness alone does not establish these laws. A checked Round rewrite
may change this provider subject to those semantic premises.
\paragraph{Admission and publication records.} Each component's \code{phases}
vector is exactly \code{[\{evaluate\}\{admit\}\{publish\}]}.
A rule's \code{insertion} operation is \code{insert}: all-column membership
admission pushes only fresh heads into that member's next container.
Evaluations read only the committed snapshot; admission never publishes an
accumulated relation or layout early.
Each \code{Publication} names a component relation and an ordered
\code{operations} vector. Each \code{PublicationOp} has a \code{kind} and
\code{layouts} indices in the relevant full or delta layout list:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{full\_layouts}: insert the fresh next rows into the listed full
layouts, using their recorded column permutations and key prefixes.
\item \code{all\_extend}: extend the accumulated host with the next rows;
its layout-index list is empty.
\item \code{delta\_swap}: replace the current delta host with the next batch;
its layout-index list is empty.
\item \code{delta\_rebuild}: reset each listed delta layout and rebuild it
from the replaced current delta, even when that batch is empty.
\end{itemize}
Publication covers every component member once. Full-layout maintenance and
accumulated extension precede that member's delta replacement; delta rebuilding
follows it. Every declared full and delta layout of the member, including an
unused layout, is maintained exactly once. Accumulated extension and delta
replacement each occur exactly once. Layout indices must reference the correct
member and version; \code{full\_layouts} and \code{delta\_rebuild} each require
a nonempty index list. Their lists may be split or reordered within the stated
dependencies, rather than having to match the default grouping.
Omitting maintenance, reusing an index, retaining a stale delta layout or
operating across these dependency boundaries is rejected.
The publication member list may be any permutation of the component:
publication is a barrier with no interleaved query evaluation, not a requirement
to repeat the default member order.

\paragraph{Schedule records.}
A Round program records one \code{schedule}: a \code{progress} mode and, per
component, each rule hole's positive \emph{read set} and an ordered list of
\emph{waves}. A read is a pair (relation, \code{full}$\mid$\code{delta}); a
hole's read set is derived from the Storage \code{Read} records of its
variant: a \code{direct} read names the relation's delta, a \code{delta}
layout read names its layout's relation at delta, a \code{full} layout read
names its layout's relation at full. Negative keys are frozen for the
component and are not reads. A wave names a phase (\code{seed} or
\code{step}) and the positions of rule holes in that phase's list. The seed
waves precede the step waves; each phase's waves partition its holes, every
hole appearing exactly once; a wave is nonempty. The only admitted progress
mode is \code{synchronous}: every hole of a round reads the snapshot
committed by the preceding publication. The schedule records which holes the
printer may evaluate concurrently and after which wave each head's admission
may begin; it records no join order, provider, or CQ decision, and Fork's
default schedule is one wave per phase containing every hole.
Two further per-component records are realization advice and a derived
placement. A \code{policy} entry names one hole at most once and may give a
positive chunk \code{grain} and an \code{inline\_below} driver length under
which that hole's own chunking is inlined; it never affects wave concurrency
or any value. A \code{publication} entry names, per phase with waves and per
member, the wave after which that member's publication may begin.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Schedule}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Round}}(P)\quad
 \forall h.\ \mathrm{reads}(h)=\mathrm{derive}(\mathrm{Storage},h)\quad
 \text{waves partition each phase's holes, nonempty, seeds before steps}\quad
 \text{policy names existing holes once, positive grain}\quad
 \mathrm{publication}=\mathrm{derive}(\mathrm{reads},\mathrm{waves})\quad
 \mathrm{progress}=\code{synchronous}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Schedule}}(P)}
\end{mathpar}
Publication of a member $H$ mutates the hosts and layouts that holes reading
$H$ (at either version) scan or probe in the same round, and consumes the
admitted rows of every hole whose head is $H$. Its placement is therefore
derived, not chosen: the last wave of the phase containing a writer or reader
of $H$, or the phase's last wave when none touches $H$. A printer that starts
$H$'s admission and publication after that wave, inside the following wave's
scope, interleaves them only with holes that neither write nor read $H$.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-PublishAfterReaders}{w_H=\max\{w\mid \text{wave }w\text{ holds }h,\ \mathrm{head}(h)=H\lor H\in\mathrm{reads}(h)\}\quad
 \text{publish}(H)\text{ starts after wave }w_H\text{ joins}}
 {\text{no hole of the round observes }H\text{ after its publication}}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Evaluations in one round read only the committed
immutable snapshot $(A_r,\Delta_r)$ and never a candidate of the same round.
Hence the candidate bag of a round is $\biguplus_h h(A_r,\Delta_r)$ for any
evaluation order, concurrency, or wave partition, and a head's admission,
which is owned by exactly one canonical owner, yields the same
$\Delta_{r+1}$ whether it runs after the whole phase or as soon as every hole
of that head has completed. Waves therefore change no value; they bound which
evaluations may overlap and when an admission may start.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-WaveCommute}{\text{holes read only }(A_r,\Delta_r)\quad
 \text{admission of head }H\text{ runs after every hole with head }H}
 {\mathrm{supp}\bigl(\textstyle\biguplus_h C_h\bigr)\text{ and }\Delta_{r+1}
 \text{ are independent of wave partition and overlap}}
\end{mathpar}
The schedule is a Round rewrite target: a \code{schedule} pass changes only
the schedule record, and the correspondence checker requires both contracts
and exact equality of the erased programs modulo that record.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Schedule}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Schedule}}(s)\quad
 \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Schedule}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{erase}(s)[\mathrm{schedule}:=\mathrm{erase}(t).\mathrm{schedule}]=\mathrm{erase}(t)}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{schedule}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Admitted wave advice: \code{whole} (one wave per phase), \code{per\_hole}
(one hole per wave, in list order), \code{by\_head} (holes grouped by head
relation in head order), or an explicit per-component listing. Advice
selects among schedules that WF-DL-Schedule already admits; it is not a
semantic choice.

\paragraph{Relational progress (proposal, not a rule).}
A \code{relational} progress mode would let a hole run round $i+1$ as soon as
every relation it reads has published round $i$, with each hole's delta
window being the admissions of its delta relation since that hole last ran,
and its full reads a snapshot no older than the window's end. The covering
argument then takes the latest-admitted tuple of a derivation: the step
variant selecting that occurrence runs with that tuple in its window and all
other tuples already in full. This needs snapshot isolation from storage
(immutable runs, or a per-relation reader/writer discipline for publication)
and a new covering obligation beside \code{seminaive\_covering}; neither is
recorded or checked here, and the base printers reject any other progress mode.

\paragraph{Control and output records.} A component's \code{termination}
list contains each component member exactly once, in any order, with no foreign
references. Continuation means that at least one listed current delta is
nonempty. The \code{termination\_test} operation records either the default
\code{sum\_delta\_lengths}, testing whether the sum of current delta lengths
is nonzero, or \code{any\_delta}, testing the disjunction of their nonemptiness.
Provider length and emptiness operations must reflect their actual rows;
the summation implementation additionally requires representable length sums
on successful executions. Either test is admitted independently of loop form.
\code{loop\_form} records either
\code{while\_nonempty} (the default) or \code{loop\_until\_empty}
with an equivalent explicit exit.
The \code{observer} placement is \code{before\_step}; when an observer is
declared, it runs before each nonempty-delta step, after seed publication and
before that step's evaluation. It receives the component-local round count and
the sum of the listed delta lengths, regardless of the termination test.
\code{key\_scope} is \code{component}:
frozen negative keys are built for their consuming component and do not escape
its lifetime. Output references preserve the exact source output relation list
and order; an out-of-range or substituted relation is rejected.
\code{borrowed\_cloned\_into\_iter} converts an EDB's canonical borrowed rows
to owned output tuples, while \code{owned\_into\_iter} consumes an IDB's
accumulated provider. Conversion must match the referenced relation kind.
The remaining \code{RoundPolicy} field is \code{timing}, accepting \code{none}
or \code{phases}; timing and observer effects remain outside relational meaning.
Provider, admission, publication, termination, observer, scope, loop and output
choices are explicit records, not decisions recovered from a bundled policy label.

\paragraph{Recorded scans.} The generic \code{ScanBinding} names one complete
immutable delta host or key-zero layout bucket as a flat CQ input, preserving
its column order and one-occurrence correspondence. The default
\code{\_\_cq\_datalog\_scan\_*} name is an ordinary Round-IR input binding,
not a parallel-specific decision: the sequential printer binds the whole
input and records no execution annotation. A later direct delta read remains
at its original body position; permitting it does not move Rust expressions
or turn it into a leading parallel scan.

\paragraph{Printer obligation.} The shared printer consumes the recorded
provider syntax and operations. It must not choose a new storage representation,
publication order, loop form or output conversion. Default producers retain
the same operations; codegen partitioning changes their Rust calling convention,
not their order. The printer
still has a refinement obligation to implement each operation correctly:
structural record checks are not an independent Rust equivalence proof.
\paragraph{Nested CQ codegen trial (historical).} The measured trial of a
partitioned pull terminal against \code{49d0ebd} did not meet the adoption gate:
the largest preoptimization CGU share remained above $51\%$, compile wall/CPU
time increased, and unchanged runtime was not established. The experimental
terminal was removed; the ordinary pull terminal remains the default.
Full measurements are recorded in
\code{features/datalog/doop/codegen-evidence.json}.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Storage exactly.
The target may change checked execution records, frozen-key provider types,
timing and each hole's CQ sub-pipeline independently; it must retain the typed
terminal registry, context facts, admission owners, runtime names and query
correspondence except for the independently checked scan-certificate substitution.
In particular, a rewrite may choose \code{stage(batch(64))} for one hole,
\code{represent(positions)} for another, or insert Structures rewrites.
These are recorded invocation arguments, not changes to the embedded CQ
meaning. A rewrite may change a hole's interface together with its pipeline
and lowering ordinal, provided both contracts independently check the selected
terminal against the same unchanged registry. This selects a registered
calling convention; it does not mutate the registry or assert a new alias.
Each selected CQ pass subsequently checks its own language and rewrite
obligations when the nested pipeline expands.
Positional representation and batching additionally require positional runtime
hosts. The example rewrite applies them to two direct-delta scan holes; a
grouped hash-map layout is not thereby made into a positional host.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-RoundRewrite}{\mathrm{WF}(R)\quad\mathrm{WF}(R')\quad
 D'=D\quad\Gamma'=\Gamma\quad
 \forall i.\ h'_i=h_i\land\mathsf{facts}'_i=\mathsf{facts}_i\quad
 E'\text{ satisfies the admitted execution obligations}}
 {R\leadsto R'}
\end{mathpar}
Here $h_i$ is compared after checked scan-certificate erasure; the corresponding
sub-pipelines $Q_i$ need not be equal. Publication and termination permutations,
the termination test and loop form need not match the source or default producer.
No canonical storage selection is substituted while checking holes.
Parallel execution is not a base flag: an extension owns partition, ownership,
barriers, and its printer. Any execution annotation needs an erasure/refinement
check, disjoint covering ownership and lawful merges. Round rewrites run before
CQ hole expansion.

\paragraph{Executable reference semantics.}
The feature-local \code{cq\_datalog\_testkit} exposes \code{eval\_source},
\code{eval\_incremental} and \code{eval\_round} as typed runtime drivers.
The corresponding \code{cq\_datalog\_test\_passes} frontends
\code{eval\_source!}, \code{eval\_incremental!} and \code{eval\_round!}
generate native Rust evaluation from their respective checked languages;
the latter two are terminal passes for explicit Incremental and Round IR.
Source evaluation uses synchronous, per-component set fixpoints. Signed dependency
rank relaxation derives its component order independently of production passes.
Incremental evaluation uses the supplied seeds and covering step variants.
Round evaluation uses the supplied execution records and embedded holes.
These references do not rerun stratify, incremental, storage or fork to recover
the default producer's chosen IR, nor use the production Datalog printer to
obtain the reference answer. Only \code{seminaive\_covering} is admitted.

Native Rust hole evaluation is deliberate: guards, bindings, finite generators,
computed heads and user row types are opaque Rust, including partial expressions
whose panic behavior depends on the reached written-order prefix.
The separate \code{cq\_testkit::cq\_semantics} evaluator enumerates an integer
fragment with variable-only positive atoms and supported total comparisons.
It supplies independent relational evidence for that fragment, not an interpreter
for arbitrary Rust holes. Replacing native holes with that scalar evaluator would
either exclude supported programs or erase the partial-execution obligation.

The drivers propagate panics; the re-exported \code{check\_refinement} harness
evaluates each side once and catches panic as the bottom outcome.
A successful reference requires equal output sets and no target panic; a reference
panic imposes no successful-result obligation and does not require a target panic.
Comparisons of Source, Incremental, Round and generated execution are per-instance
evidence for pass and rewrite premises, not proofs of equivalence, prefix safety,
termination, or provider laws. Structural acceptance alone provides none of these
semantic proofs. Purity, lawful equality, hashing and cloning, finite generators
and termination remain assumptions; observers are excluded from relational
denotation. Divergence has no timeout-based denotation.

\section{Datalog passes and crate organization}
Each language crate owns \code{Program: Parse + ToTokens + Language}, its
\code{contract::check} analysis and \code{check\_rewrite}. Each nonterminal
pass library exports compilation and an independent correspondence check;
the check must not call compilation. Compilation owns that check; thin macro
wrappers do not repeat it. They use \code{cq\_stage::pass} or
\code{cq\_stage::rewrite}, whose admitted rewrite path reuses the contracts
already checked by the runner. Standalone \code{check\_rewrite} still checks
both operands. A Datalog-owned
\code{DatalogConfiguration} resolves named phases to a generic
\code{Pipeline}; it does not modify the CQ configuration or core crates.
Direct pass entry uses the same arguments/remaining-pipeline protocol.

\subsection{Surface if-let: tokens to tokens}
Expand a matched pattern into the existing \code{for}/\code{match} generator
at the same written position; the scrutinee is evaluated once per reached
prefix and a mismatch yields no rows. Preserve bound variables and unowned
syntax. This is an expansion check, not a Source parser side effect.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-IfLet}{t\text{ preserves the matched binders, scrutinee and position}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{iflet}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement is conditional on the ordinary opaque-Rust premises.

\subsection{Stratify: Source to Strata}
Construct the deterministic dependency SCC order. Independently check both
contracts and exact source preservation; the target need only satisfy the
admissible component contract, not reproduce that deterministic order.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Stratify}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{source}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{stratify}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement follows from freezing lower components, subject to the common
termination and Rust premises.

\subsection{Incremental: Strata to Incremental}
Construct fresh delta declarations and the default covering expansion.
Independently check exact base preservation, erasure, coverage and substitutions;
do not compare target names or variant order with a regenerated expansion.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Incremental}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{strata}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{incremental}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The total finite refinement uses DL-Delta and DL-FrozenNegation; partial Rust
requires the additional prefix-safety obligation, not merely this check.

\subsection{Storage: Incremental to Storage}
Record the existing deterministic concrete providers, initializers, layouts,
bindings and maintenance. Independently check exact base preservation and the
target's admissible storage decisions, without reconstructing the default plan
or whitelisting concrete Rust types.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Storage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{incremental}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{storage}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement requires the stated $\alpha$ laws for the selected providers.

\subsection{Fork: Storage to Round}
Construct bound CQ holes and explicit operational records, copying provider
types, initializers and layout maintenance exactly. Carry a sub-pipeline and
typed interface for each hole and the configuration's complete typed terminal
registry. Independently check variant-to-hole correspondence, frozen-key
construction, storage preservation, terminal declarations, publication
dependencies and coverage, termination and output references. Alternative
publication permutations or loop forms need not equal Fork's default output.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Fork}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Round}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{storage}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{fork}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement is a storage-state simulation with the same partial-Rust premises.

\subsection{Print rounds: Round to Rust}
The sequential terminal printer interprets recorded bindings, provider types,
initializers, admission and publication operations, loop and termination
records, observers and output conversions, plus one nested CQ macro invocation
per hole. It never prints joins or substitutes a default choice for a supplied
record. CQ's selected terminal printer expands each hole later. Shared Datalog
printing operations live in \code{cq-datalog-print}; extension printers reuse
them through phase evaluation and alternative publication realizations.
The terminal puts each component body and each consumed rule evaluation behind
a separately monomorphized codegen partitioning helper. Generated arguments are
shared references to read hosts and exclusive references to written storage;
round-local next buffers stay local to the component. Host selection intersects
the recorded storage names with references in the emitted body, and mutation
comes from component membership/publication or the rule's admission head.
User Rust expressions remain in lexical closures, so host expressions, local
types, helper closures, and observers need not be expressible as item captures.
The closures are \code{inline(always)} inside \code{inline(never)} helpers.
There is no allocation, dynamic dispatch, row copying, or new parallel runtime
execution at these boundaries.

Rustc deliberately ignores modules nested inside functions when assigning
codegen units. Therefore the hidden helpers live in sixteen module-level
partitions in the Datalog facade, not in modules nested in the macro expansion.
Hole numbers and subsequent component numbers select these partitions modulo
sixteen; distinct closure types yield distinct helper monomorphizations.
This is a fixed terminal realization, not a new Round planning option.
The parallel terminal uses the same helpers around its existing scheduling and
publication, with observers still on the evaluating thread.
Nested CQ iterator/closure monomorphizations can still concentrate in the
caller's module; these helpers do not claim completely balanced preoptimization
IR. The core CQ compiler and selected CQ terminal remain unchanged.

As with core terminal printers, there is no independent Rust equivalence
checker: contract checks, operation-fixture comparison after erasing only the
borrowed dispatch envelopes, and executable oracle agreement supply evidence,
not a general Rust refinement proof.

\paragraph{Default and alternative hash-provider selection.}
Datalog directly records unkeyed Fx providers for Storage's native layout
maps, IDB membership sets, EDB canonical sets, and Round's negation key sets.
The discharged default holes have no CQ dedup representations, so their queue
installs no \code{retype(dedup => ...)} rewrite. An explicit dedup-provider
override is likewise invalid when no such representation remains; it must not
be retained as a no-op compatibility setting.
No MiniLinq core defaults are changed. The facade reexports
\code{rustc\_hash}, so default consumers need no new direct dependency for Fx.
The public \code{cq-datalog-hasher} plug-in retains explicit

These rewrites are parallel-agnostic and are not installed in the default queue.
Fx uses \code{BuildHasherDefault<rustc\_hash::FxHasher>} from version 2.1.3,
as does the Ascent oracle. All providers require lawful hashing and equality.
The default additionally assumes trusted input: unkeyed Fx is not
collision-attack resistant. Core fallback index directories and core-internal
sets are not rewritten.

\subsection{Parallel partition: Round to extension-owned annotated Round}
The separate extension accepts an unannotated source with sharded admission,
checks both Round contracts, and requires exact erasure of the embedded target
Round to the source after restoring only the independently checked terminal
substitution below. All other policies and per-hole CQ queue entries are preserved.
It records
exactly one annotation per eligible rule scan, in component seed/step order,
selecting occurrence zero and strategy \code{rayon\_sharded}. The Round
certificate checks a fresh direct scan binding and one renamed leading
positive occurrence; negative key sets are not partitioned.
The extension requires vector-backed delta inputs and a native layout's
contiguous vector bucket for a key-zero scan. Opaque aliases and non-vector
containers remain admissible in base Storage but are not supported by this
extension.
The renamed scan's host certificate is derived from the certified original
host: indexed chunks are disjoint slices of the same immutable complete rows,
so each chunk remains duplicate-free. The extension preserves the per-hole
consumer and discharged-host facts under this checked renaming, along with
their exact CQ context envelopes and all authored pass arguments.
For rule holes only, partition may replace the exact registered standard
\code{::mini\_linq::passes::translate\_pull} terminal with empty arguments by
the registered \code{::cq\_datalog::passes::print\_push} terminal and change
the interface from Pull to Push. The registry itself is unchanged. A missing
push registration, custom terminal path or authored terminal arguments preserve
the original invocation. Frozen-key holes are untouched. This choice applies
to scan and no-scan rules independently of the self-deduplication policy:
push feeds either the task-local collector or the candidate vector directly.
The correspondence checker restores only this permitted source terminal and
interface before demanding exact equality after scan erasure; a general
unregistered terminal replacement is not an admitted partition rewrite.
The extension's \code{ShardCertificate.self\_dedup: Option<Ident>} records
its candidate-deduplication policy. Absence retains the baseline stream;
the only admitted present value is \code{per\_task}. Each partitioned scan's
\code{Execution.self\_dedup} repeats this policy as a checked witness,
admissible only with \code{rayon\_sharded}. The policy covers every rule
evaluation: a rule without a partitionable scan is one task. Frozen-key holes
are excluded. Task-local deduplication precedes global admission, using
\code{HashTable<usize>} indices into an owned candidate \code{Vec} and the
existing admission hasher. It preserves first-occurrence order and returns
the vector directly, without cloning rows or introducing CQ's
\code{\_\_cq\_seen}. The collector alone does not eliminate a retained pull
terminal's result materialization; the checked standard-terminal substitution
instead streams push candidates directly into the selected task collector.
The checker validates the policy and matching scan witnesses before erasing
these realization records; erasure leaves the CQ query, \code{Hole.facts},
and admission owner unchanged. For task candidate bags $B_j$, the justification is
\[
 \mathrm{supp}\!\left(\biguplus_j \mathrm{supp}(B_j)\right)
 =\mathrm{supp}\!\left(\biguplus_j B_j\right).
\]
This optimization does not establish global freshness: equal heads from
different tasks, rules or prior rounds still require the same canonical
admission owner. It neither restores CQ \code{return canon} nor adds a
sequential hole-local set. Candidate multiplicities and insertion-attempt counts
may change; relational support and the checked context envelope do not.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Partition}{\mathrm{restoreTerminals}_s(\mathrm{erase}(t.\mathrm{round}))=s\quad
 s\text{ is unannotated, with certified admission and native layout providers}\quad
 t\text{ has exact ordered scan certificates and valid self-dedup modes}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{partition}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The printer partitions the same immutable leading scan into indexed Rayon
chunks with grain $\max(g,\lfloor n/(4\,\mathrm{threads})\rfloor)$, where
$g$ is the recorded minimum grain (default 64). Inline evaluation requires
both total member delta size and the sum of driving scan lengths over holes
to be below the recorded threshold (default 1024); DOOP selects 64 explicitly.
A one-worker pool also evaluates inline. Thus a small delta cannot serialize
an expensive full-input hole. This avoids narrow-delta task overhead without changing
the selected occurrence or CQ rule. A different occurrence would require a
new checked rule; it is not an unchecked performance choice.

The extension printer realizes the recorded schedule. The partition pass
preserves the schedule record exactly. Each wave is one joining Rayon scope
whose tasks are that wave's holes. Inside a hole, chunking of the leading
scan uses the hole's recorded \code{grain} and is inlined when the driver is
shorter than its recorded \code{inline\_below}; the certificate's threshold
and grain are the defaults. The wave itself is spawned whenever the pool has
more than one worker: a small delta inlines a hole's chunks, never the
concurrency between holes. A head's sharded admission is spawned into the
first scope after the last wave containing one of its holes. When the
recorded publication wave of a member precedes the phase's last wave, that
member's admission and complete publication (full layouts, accumulated
extension, delta replacement, delta rebuilds, in the recorded order) run as
one task in the following wave's scope and are omitted from the phase
barrier; otherwise they follow the final wave as before. The sequential base
printer evaluates holes in wave order with its inline admission and ignores
policy and placement. Neither printer accepts a progress mode other than
\code{synchronous}.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{P-DL-Waves}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Schedule}}(t)\quad
 \text{scope}_w\text{ joins before scope}_{w+1}\quad
 \mathrm{admit}(H)\text{ is spawned after the last wave of }H\text{'s holes}\quad
 \mathrm{publish}(H)\text{ is spawned after }w_H\text{ (DL-PublishAfterReaders)}}
 {\text{the realization refines DL-WaveCommute}}
\end{mathpar}

\subsection{Sharded admission and exclusive-layout publication (extension)}
The extension's Storage rewrite replaces only canonical IDB membership;
full/delta layout maps, all other Storage records and CQ queues are unchanged.
A Round-to-extension pass records the small-work threshold, partition grain,
admission shard-count policy and ownership/publication certificate. None of
these parallel choices belongs to the base language or default expansion.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-ShardStorage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(s)\quad
 \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(t)\quad
 t\text{ changes only canonical IDB membership providers}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{shardStorage}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Let $S$ be a power of two at least the worker count and let
$h_S(x)=h(x)\mathbin{\&}(S-1)$. Canonical admission uses the full tuple:
$C_i=\{r\in C\mid h_S(r)=i\}$, so equal tuples always share a shard.
The recorded membership provider's build hasher supplies $h$ for both routing
and the shard table. The runtime carries the computed hash with each candidate
into its shard and reuses it for admission; table growth may rehash stored rows.
Random-seeded providers need not share routing across distinct instances.
The Storage correspondence check preserves an explicitly recorded hasher when
substituting \code{ShardedSeen<Tuple, Hasher>}; native layout maps may record
any build hasher while retaining their checked key and vector-bucket types.
Each shard owns its \code{seen} set exclusively and inserts candidates once;
its newly admitted rows form $N_i$. Concatenation is in shard order, but
output order is unspecified. The termination condition is any admitted row.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-ShardedAdmission}{N_i=C_i\setminus A_i\quad A'_i=A_i\cup C_i
 \quad 0\leq i<S}
 {\mathrm{set}(\mathrm{concat}_{i=0}^{S-1}N_i)=C\setminus A}
\end{mathpar}
Each native layout has exactly one owner task during publication. Full layouts
extend their previous state; after all full-layout tasks join, relation
containers extend and deltas swap. A second scope replaces each delta map with
its recorded default provider and rebuilds it, including empty deltas, then joins before evaluation.
The original \code{IndexRead}/\code{Rows} implementations serve CQ holes
without fallback index construction or a shard-routing hash on every probe.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-LayoutPublication}{\mathrm{owner}\text{ is a bijection on layouts}\quad
 L'_\ell=L_\ell\cup\mathrm{layout}_\ell(N)\quad
 \text{all full owners join before delta replacement}}
 {\forall\ell.\ \alpha(L'_\ell)=\alpha(L_\ell)\cup\mathrm{layout}_\ell(N)}
\Rule{CK-DL-ShardOwnership}{
 \{[i,i+1)\mid 0\leq i<S\}\text{ disjointly covers admission shards}\quad
 \mathrm{owner}\text{ is total and injective on each full/delta layout domain}\quad
 \text{every required layout completes before evaluation}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{shardedRound}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The checker validates admission's symbolic shard ownership and whole-layout
owner bijections directly, without invoking the certificate producer. It checks
complete membership/coverage and each stratum's publication barriers. The printer
uses certified layout lists and owner labels to generate and order its tasks.
Reordered certificates and valid owner permutations are accepted. Scoped tasks
receive exclusive mutable seen shards or native maps, never locks. Below
the recorded candidate threshold, admission runs inline while preserving shard
concatenation order; publication similarly avoids tasks for small next sets.
The base Round admission-owner lists remain checked independently of the
extension's shard and layout owner certificates. A candidate buffer may contain
duplicate heads from one hole, several holes or several workers; equal complete
tuples route to the same admission shard and only fresh tuples enter next.
Consequently sharded admission discharges the same \code{consumer canonical}
fact, and exclusive-layout publication preserves \textsc{DL-HostCanonical}
for the subsequent snapshot. Optional \code{per\_task} execution deduplication
is only a candidate-volume optimization, not a CQ-owned canonical boundary.
Sequential holes remain free of local dedup state, and neither execution mode
weakens the exact context-envelope requirement.
Purity, lawful hashing/equality/cloning and the partial-Rust prefix premises
remain required. Neither ownership checking nor the counters regression is a
general Rust equivalence proof.
The base printing library exports generic \code{full\_insert},
\code{all\_extend}, \code{delta\_swap} and \code{delta\_rebuild} generators.
Both terminals reuse those operations; the extension changes only their
grouping into checked phases. Delta rebuilds use default replacement in both
terminals, preserving the base expansion rather than silently using \code{clear}.
The extension printer's default-off \code{hole-timing} feature emits per-hole
and per-chunk evaluation times, input/candidate counts, partition status and
the erased CQ rule text. No timers, atomics or logging are emitted without
that feature. Chunk time excludes record emission; hole wall time includes
chunk-record output, so diagnostic times are not performance benchmark times.

\paragraph{Measured design choice.}
The initial 1024-row minimum grain left the dominant cast/points-to hole with
only about 3.2 chunks per round in avrora and 4.3 in xalan. At 15 workers,
the maximum chunk accounted for about 50\% and 33\% of its aggregate work,
respectively. The same leading occurrence can therefore be split more finely;
these observations do not establish a memory-bandwidth limit.
The rejected sharded-layout prototype added a routing hash before each native
map hash. A corrected fast-hash prototype with threshold 64 took 1.052, 1.916,
2.779 and 1.783 seconds in phase-only runs for cassandra, avrora, xalan and
tomcat at 15 workers. The selected native-layout hybrid took 0.860, 1.684,
2.529 and 1.161 seconds respectively, retaining sharded admission while
removing the extra probe hash. These are diagnostic phase-only observations,
not the isolated uninstrumented acceptance timings. The hybrid's counters
regression observes 22000 native probes in both sequential and parallel
executions and zero fallback builds. The rejected index provider is removed,
not retained as an unmeasured default or compatibility path.
