\section{Datalog language boundaries and contracts}
\label{sec:datalog-languages}
This specification separates five checked languages above the unchanged CQ
compiler. Structural checks do not discharge all semantic obligations.
Contracts admit recorded choices; default producers choose deterministically.
The base admits dependency-ordered unions of SCCs, alternative usable layouts
and opaque concrete Rust providers, while retaining the
\code{seminaive\_covering} evaluation scheme and a checked operational vocabulary.
Structural checks examine the supplied IR and never rerun a producer to compare
with its chosen output.
The default generated Rust must remain byte-identical to the captured baseline.

\paragraph{Semantic domain and limits.}
Each relation declares a model slot $M$; the base accepts only \code{set}.
EDB hosts denote sets even when their physical traversal contains duplicates.
Outputs denote sets, not their iteration order. Rust expressions are opaque,
pure partial functions, with panic represented by $\bot$; purity and lawful
provider operations are premises, not syntactic checks. A terminating,
successful source denotes the stratified least fixpoint. Divergence has no
completed result. Computed heads and finite generators do not by themselves
guarantee a finite reachable domain. Refinement requires that a successful
reference yield the same sets without a new panic; it does not require equal
panic domains. Observers and their evaluation counts are outside this relational
meaning. A different model requires a dialect meaning and a checked translation;
no pass may silently change $M$.

\subsection{DL-Surface}
\paragraph{Grammar.} Raw tokens containing source syntax and supported sugar.
\paragraph{Contract.} Surface passes check their own expansion; the generic
raw-token language has no Datalog well-formedness judgment.
\paragraph{Meaning.} Meaning is that of the translated DL-Source program.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} None; surface syntax is eliminated before
source parsing. In particular, parsing a later IR must not conditionally
expand \code{if let}.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Preserve unowned syntax and validate the owned
expansion, including its binders and evaluation position.

\subsection{DL-Source}
\paragraph{Grammar.} Declarations (EDB bindings or IDB declarations, each with
model \code{set}), ordered rules, outputs, and an optional observer. Bodies
contain positive atoms, safe negative atoms, and written-order CQ Rust steps
including \code{let}, \code{for}, and guards; heads may be computed.
\paragraph{Contract.} Declared names, arities, types and outputs are consistent;
head and negative variables are safely bound; signed dependencies are
stratifiable. Aggregates and nested blocks remain outside this base language.
Opaque Rust must not hide reads of evolving IDBs.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Source}{\text{source safety, declarations and signed stratifiability}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Source}}(P)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Evaluate components in dependency order, taking the
inflationary least fixpoint of their immediate consequences, with lower
components fixed. The total finite theorem in the incremental section applies
under its premises; partial execution additionally has the obligations below.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Relation model, EDB bindings, written rule and
Rust-step order, outputs and observer. No schedule or physical choices.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both Source contracts and preserve the declared
interface: program name, relation declarations and models, EDB bindings, outputs
and observer.
Rules may change, including opaque Rust payloads. Preservation of relational
meaning and partial-execution refinement is a semantic premise of such a rewrite,
validated by executable refinement cases, not decided by the structural gate.

\subsection{DL-Strata}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Source plus an ordered list of components.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Source, then require a complete, nonoverlapping
partition of the IDBs into nonempty components, each a union of whole dependency
SCCs. A positive IDB dependency is in the same or an earlier component; a negative
IDB dependency is strictly earlier. Thus no component contains internal negation.
EDBs remain fixed inputs. Independent components may be reordered and positive
SCCs may be merged when these conditions hold.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Strata}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Source}}(P)\quad
 C\text{ completely partitions SCCs and respects signed dependency order}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(P,C)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Sequential composition of component fixpoints, equal to
the source meaning under the stratification premises.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Component membership and order.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Source exactly;
the target may choose a different admissible component partition or order.
The SCC theorem in the incremental section extends to each such union: its internal dependencies
are positive, and all negative inputs are frozen.

\subsection{DL-Incremental}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Strata plus fresh delta declarations and ordered
seed/step variants, each identifying its source rule and delta occurrence.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Strata and accept only the recorded scheme
\code{seminaive\_covering}. Check fresh delta names and matching types, rule
identity, variant erasure and changing-position coverage directly. Each rule
with no positive occurrence in its current component has at least one seed;
otherwise each changing positive occurrence is selected by at least one step
variant reading its delta, while other changing occurrences read the accumulated
relation. Preserve head, written body order, Rust steps and fixed inputs under
erasure. Repeated seed and step variants are allowed: covering is not a disjoint
or exactly-once derivation partition. Variant order and fresh names need not equal
the default producer's choices.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Incremental}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(S)\quad
 V\text{ has valid deltas, erasure and seminaive covering coverage}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(S,V)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} Starting from the deduplicated seeds, each round reads
immutable accumulated $A_r$ and delta $\Delta_r$, computes candidates $C_r$,
and admits and publishes
\[
 \Delta_{r+1}=\mathrm{supp}(C_r)\setminus A_r,\qquad
 A_{r+1}=A_r\cup\Delta_{r+1}.
\]
Stop when all component deltas are empty. These are set operations; the model
slot does not authorize lattice subtraction or semiring differences.
\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Covering semi-naive scheme, delta names,
variant identities and occurrence substitutions. Naive and disjoint exact
schemes are not accepted merely because they are conceivable alternatives.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve the underlying
Strata program exactly; delta declarations and variant schedules may change
provided the target independently satisfies erasure and coverage.

\paragraph{Frozen negation.}
Let $B$ contain completed lower-component relations. Within the consuming
component, a negative literal is a fixed predicate of the currently bound
values, not a changing positive position. It is neither delta-substituted nor
counted toward coverage. For total pure rules, the usual covering argument
therefore applies with $B$ held fixed:
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-FrozenNegation}{B\text{ is complete and immutable}\quad
 q_B\text{ is total and monotone in its positive current-component inputs}}
 {q_B(R\cup\Delta)=q_B(R)\cup\bigcup_i V_{B,i}(R\cup\Delta,\Delta)}
\end{mathpar}
Wildcards are existentially projected out of negative membership keys;
constant filters remain part of key construction. No rule above permits
negation of a changing current-component relation.

\paragraph{Partial Rust across rounds.}
Erasure, body-order preservation and full-valuation coverage do not prove
prefix safety: a partial expression can run on a prefix whose later atom is
empty. The additional obligation is that every prefix evaluated by every
generated variant is safe whenever the written-order reference succeeds,
including seed execution and cross-round repetition. Per-hole CQ checks and
Before facts constrain each call but do not discharge this multi-round
obligation. The total finite fixpoint theorem is not claimed for arbitrary
partial Rust. Executable panic/refinement cases provide evidence, not a proof
for all opaque expressions.

\subsection{DL-Storage}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Incremental plus a declaration-ordered
\code{providers} vector, full and delta layouts, occurrence-to-read bindings,
frozen negative projections, and insertion/negation policies. Each
\code{Providers} record contains a local \code{canonical\_name}, the opaque Rust
type \code{canonical\_set} with generic row parameter \code{R}, and concrete
row-container types \code{all}, \code{seen}, \code{delta} and \code{next}.
The opaque expressions \code{all\_init}, \code{delta\_init} and
\code{next\_init} initialize their respective containers. Each \code{Layout}
records its relation, complete column permutation, key-prefix length,
opaque concrete map \code{provider} type and \code{maintenance} operation.
A \code{Read} records the variant, written body position, read kind, structure
index and column order. A \code{Negative} records its consuming component,
source relation, projected columns and constant filters.

\paragraph{Textual records.} Storage and Round use the existing
\code{record!} grammar, not a second surface language: fields occur in their
declared order as \code{field \{ payload \}}; a vector is a bracketed sequence
of braced entries without commas; an option is \code{[]} or
\code{[payload]}. Thus a two-member index vector is \code{[\{0\}\{1\}]}.
Rust types and expressions are parsed losslessly as opaque Rust syntax inside
their field delimiters. Parse/print roundtripping retains these choices;
resuming a checked IR does not consult a producer to recover them.

\paragraph{Contract.} Check Incremental, require one provider record per source
declaration, and accept the supported all-column insertion and borrowed
negation policies \code{all\_columns} and
\code{borrowed}. Every full layout records
\code{admitted\_rows} maintenance; every delta layout records
\code{replace\_each\_run}. Each declared layout has a complete column
permutation and valid key prefix, including additional unused layouts.
For every recorded read, locate the actual source-rule occurrence and derive
the variables already bound at that written position and its constants.
The selected structure must belong to the correct relation and version, and
its key must use only available columns; a recorded bound-column claim is not
evidence. Require exactly one binding per positive occurrence and no extraneous
bindings, together with the prescribed frozen negative projection/filter,
including wildcard treatment. A \code{direct} binding reads the selected
delta relation in declaration column order; it need not be the first body
occurrence. Full and delta layouts must serve their respective versions.
The checker does not whitelist applicable Rust provider types or interpret their
initializer expressions; typing and operation availability are checked by Rust.
It does require syntactically identical \code{delta} and \code{next} types,
because \code{delta\_swap} moves the entire next container into delta.
The uniform provider record reserves fields for both relation kinds. For an
EDB, the unused \code{all}, \code{seen}, \code{delta}, \code{next} and their
initializers must retain the default row-container records. For an IDB,
the unused \code{canonical\_name} and \code{canonical\_set} must retain their
defaults. Changes to these inapplicable fields are rejected, not silently ignored.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Storage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(I)\quad
 D\text{ records complete providers, usable reads and maintained layouts}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(I,D)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning and provider premises.} Data refinement by $\alpha$ requires
the EDB canonical provider and each published full layout to represent the
same relation, delta layouts to represent precisely the current fresh delta,
and negative keys to represent the prescribed projection/filter of frozen
inputs. Initializers and default construction must denote empty containers.
In particular, membership sets, initial full/delta layout hosts, and every
delta-layout reset use \code{::core::default::Default::default()}.
Default construction is part of these operations' meaning, not an independent
initializer choice: each selected provider must implement \code{Default} as
the empty represented collection, including every empty delta-run rebuild.
Canonical collection removes duplicates without changing row values; canonical
iteration and cloning preserve those values. The membership provider's
\code{insert} returns true exactly for a previously absent complete row,
and retains that row. Sequence push, extension, traversal and owned iteration
preserve their rows; moving the next container to delta preserves exactly the
admitted batch. Map entry lookup and bucket insertion implement the recorded
key/payload split, without losing rows or mixing unequal keys. Equality,
hashing, ordering (where required), cloning and borrowing obey the relevant
provider laws, and these operations introduce no new panic on successful
reference executions. These are semantic premises, not proofs obtained by
parsing a type name or by checking method availability.

\paragraph{Defaults and checked alternatives.} The default canonical wrapper
is named \code{Canonical}, with \code{::cq\_storage::HashSet<R>} storage.
The accumulated, delta and next providers are concrete \code{Vec} row types,
initialized with \code{::std::vec::Vec::new()}; membership uses
\code{::cq\_storage::HashSet} of complete rows. Layouts use
\code{::std::collections::HashMap} from key tuples to \code{Vec} payload
tuples, with \code{::cq\_storage::HashState}. These are default records, not
the definition of admissibility. A concrete \code{BTreeMap} layout with the
same key/payload types is admitted under its lawful ordering premise.
Likewise a later changing occurrence may select a direct delta scan instead
of a delta layout, retaining its written position and declaration-order columns.

\paragraph{Recorded decisions.} Concrete provider types, wrapper name,
container initializers, layout shape and maintenance, occurrence bindings,
negative projections and the supported insertion/negation operations.
Applicable opaque provider syntax is retained through Fork and consumed by the
printer, rather than replaced with default types; inapplicable fields are
checked reserved defaults.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Incremental exactly;
allow changed provider records, layouts and occurrence bindings when the target
satisfies coverage, key availability, version and maintenance obligations.
Provider refinement remains a semantic premise of such a rewrite.

\subsection{DL-Round}
\paragraph{Grammar.} DL-Storage plus a \code{RoundContext} of runtime
relations, copied concrete providers and layouts, component \code{Stratum}
records, outputs and observer bindings; complete embedded CQ holes; and a
CQ sub-pipeline and typed interface for each hole. A \code{Stratum} records
members, component-local negative keys, ordered seed/step \code{RuleHole}
lists, explicit phases, publication operations, termination references and test,
observer placement, key scope and loop form. Each \code{RuleHole} records the variant, head,
hole index, optional scan binding and membership \code{insertion} operation.
An \code{Output} records a relation reference and conversion operation.
The program also records the complete \code{pull\_printers} allowlist of
exact macro paths.
\code{HoleInterface::Pull} denotes an iterator result consumed by Round.
\code{HoleInterface::Push} is representable in text for a clear unsupported
interface diagnostic, but no base Round consumer implements it.
\paragraph{Contract.} Check Storage, exact copied provider/layout correspondence,
hole correspondence under the recorded reads, seed/step ordering, fresh runtime
names and the operational obligations below. The check does not reconstruct a
default storage or Round plan. Each hole must record the pull interface and an
argument-free terminal invocation whose exact path is in its recorded allowlist.
The default allowlist is exactly
\code{::mini\_linq::passes::print\_pull} and
\code{mini\_linq::passes::print\_pull}; leading qualification is significant.
For example, \code{pull\_printers = [crate::alias::pull];} in
\code{DatalogConfiguration}, together with
\code{cq = pipeline \{ print = crate::alias::pull; \};}, explicitly replaces
that list. Fork serializes every declaration, including unused ones, into
Round text, so the contract checks a resumed Round without consulting the
configuration or invoking compilation. Unlisted terminals (including the
default push printer) and typed push holes are rejected.
Declaring an alternate path is the caller's assertion of its pull calling
convention, not Rust alias resolution or a proof of printer equivalence.
This check examines terminal invocations, not Rust tokens that do not yet exist.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{WF-DL-Round}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(D)\quad
 H\text{ implements the recorded variants and frozen keys}\quad
 E\text{ has complete ordered maintenance, termination and outputs}}
 {\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Round}}(D,H,E)}
\end{mathpar}
\paragraph{Meaning.} A state machine over storage: evaluations see the
committed snapshot; admission rejects known and duplicate candidate heads;
publication extends full structures and replaces every delta layout, including
empty ones, before subsequent evaluation. Under provider and partial-Rust
premises its $\alpha$-image refines the Incremental transition system.

\paragraph{Frozen-key providers.} Each \code{KeySet} records its source relation,
projected columns, filters, runtime name, construction hole, borrowed
representation (\code{KeyRepresentation::Borrowed}, text \code{borrowed})
and opaque Rust \code{provider} type. The default is
\code{::cq\_storage::HashSet<\_>}; this is not a type whitelist.
Collection and borrowed membership must implement precisely the recorded
projection/filter set, with lawful equality and any required hashing or ordering.
Type correctness alone does not establish these laws. A checked Round rewrite
may change this provider subject to those semantic premises.
\paragraph{Admission and publication records.} Each component's \code{phases}
vector is exactly \code{[\{evaluate\}\{admit\}\{publish\}]}.
A rule's \code{insertion} operation is \code{insert}: all-column membership
admission pushes only fresh heads into that member's next container.
Evaluations read only the committed snapshot; admission never publishes an
accumulated relation or layout early.
Each \code{Publication} names a component relation and an ordered
\code{operations} vector. Each \code{PublicationOp} has a \code{kind} and
\code{layouts} indices in the relevant full or delta layout list:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{full\_layouts}: insert the fresh next rows into the listed full
layouts, using their recorded column permutations and key prefixes.
\item \code{all\_extend}: extend the accumulated host with the next rows;
its layout-index list is empty.
\item \code{delta\_swap}: replace the current delta host with the next batch;
its layout-index list is empty.
\item \code{delta\_rebuild}: reset each listed delta layout and rebuild it
from the replaced current delta, even when that batch is empty.
\end{itemize}
Publication covers every component member once. Full-layout maintenance and
accumulated extension precede that member's delta replacement; delta rebuilding
follows it. Every declared full and delta layout of the member, including an
unused layout, is maintained exactly once. Accumulated extension and delta
replacement each occur exactly once. Layout indices must reference the correct
member and version; \code{full\_layouts} and \code{delta\_rebuild} each require
a nonempty index list. Their lists may be split or reordered within the stated
dependencies, rather than having to match the default grouping.
Omitting maintenance, reusing an index, retaining a stale delta layout or
operating across these dependency boundaries is rejected.
The publication member list may be any permutation of the component:
publication is a barrier with no interleaved query evaluation, not a requirement
to repeat the default member order.

\paragraph{Control and output records.} A component's \code{termination}
list contains each component member exactly once, in any order, with no foreign
references. Continuation means that at least one listed current delta is
nonempty. The \code{termination\_test} operation records either the default
\code{sum\_delta\_lengths}, testing whether the sum of current delta lengths
is nonzero, or \code{any\_delta}, testing the disjunction of their nonemptiness.
Provider length and emptiness operations must reflect their actual rows;
the summation implementation additionally requires representable length sums
on successful executions. Either test is admitted independently of loop form.
\code{loop\_form} records either
\code{while\_nonempty} (the default) or \code{loop\_until\_empty}
with an equivalent explicit exit.
The \code{observer} placement is \code{before\_step}; when an observer is
declared, it runs before each nonempty-delta step, after seed publication and
before that step's evaluation. It receives the component-local round count and
the sum of the listed delta lengths, regardless of the termination test.
\code{key\_scope} is \code{component}:
frozen negative keys are built for their consuming component and do not escape
its lifetime. Output references preserve the exact source output relation list
and order; an out-of-range or substituted relation is rejected.
\code{borrowed\_cloned\_into\_iter} converts an EDB's canonical borrowed rows
to owned output tuples, while \code{owned\_into\_iter} consumes an IDB's
accumulated provider. Conversion must match the referenced relation kind.
The remaining \code{RoundPolicy} field is \code{timing}, accepting \code{none}
or \code{phases}; timing and observer effects remain outside relational meaning.
Provider, admission, publication, termination, observer, scope, loop and output
choices are explicit records, not decisions recovered from a bundled policy label.

\paragraph{Recorded scans.} The generic \code{ScanBinding} names one complete
immutable delta host or key-zero layout bucket as a flat CQ input, preserving
its column order and one-occurrence correspondence. The default
\code{\_\_cq\_datalog\_scan\_*} name is an ordinary Round-IR input binding,
not a parallel-specific decision: the sequential printer binds the whole
input and records no execution annotation. A later direct delta read remains
at its original body position; permitting it does not move Rust expressions
or turn it into a leading parallel scan.

\paragraph{Printer obligation.} The shared printer consumes the recorded
provider syntax and operations. It must not choose a new storage representation,
publication order, loop form or output conversion. Default producers record the
old choices so the default Rust expansion remains byte-identical. The printer
still has a refinement obligation to implement each operation correctly:
structural record checks are not an independent Rust equivalence proof.
\paragraph{Rewrite check.} Check both contracts and preserve Storage exactly.
The target may change checked execution records, frozen-key provider types,
timing and per-hole CQ pipelines; it must retain hole interfaces, runtime names
and query correspondence except for the independently checked scan-certificate
substitution. In particular, publication and termination permutations, the
termination test and loop form need not match the source or the default producer.
No canonical storage selection is
substituted while checking holes. Parallel execution is not a base flag:
an extension owns partition, ownership, barriers, and its printer. Any execution
annotation needs an erasure/refinement check, disjoint covering ownership and
lawful merges. Round rewrites run before CQ hole expansion.

\paragraph{Executable reference semantics.}
The feature-local Datalog testkit uses a synchronous, per-component set fixpoint
driver. Its \code{eval\_source} takes an initializer, a component count and a typed
step callback returning whether publication changed the state. The driver returns
the final state and propagates panics; the re-exported \code{check\_refinement}
harness evaluates each side once and catches panic as the bottom outcome.
The test-pass \code{eval\_source!} translates checked Source rules into
written-order native Rust loops over typed set stores, publishing candidate sets
synchronously. Signed dependency rank relaxation derives reference strata
independently of production passes. This executes opaque guards, bindings, generators and
computed heads as Rust rather than pretending to interpret them in a restricted
scalar evaluator. It does not rerun stratify, incremental, storage or fork to
obtain the reference answer.
Executable refinement compares successful reference output sets with the target
and rejects a new target panic; a reference panic imposes no successful-result
obligation. This is instance-level evidence for rewrite and pass premises, not
a decision procedure for arbitrary Rust equivalence. Purity, lawful equality,
hashing and cloning, finite generators and termination remain assumptions;
observers are excluded from relational denotation. Divergence has no timeout-based denotation. This reference
does not claim a general interpreter for Incremental or Round.

\section{Datalog passes and crate organization}
Each language crate owns \code{Program: Parse + ToTokens + Language}, its
\code{contract::check} analysis and \code{check\_rewrite}. Each nonterminal
pass library exports compilation and an independent correspondence check;
the check must not call compilation. Thin macro wrappers use
\code{cq\_stage::pass} or \code{cq\_stage::rewrite}. A Datalog-owned
\code{DatalogConfiguration} resolves named phases to a generic
\code{Pipeline}; it does not modify the CQ configuration or core crates.
Direct pass entry uses the same arguments/remaining-pipeline protocol.

\subsection{Surface if-let: tokens to tokens}
Expand a matched pattern into the existing \code{for}/\code{match} generator
at the same written position; the scrutinee is evaluated once per reached
prefix and a mismatch yields no rows. Preserve bound variables and unowned
syntax. This is an expansion check, not a Source parser side effect.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-IfLet}{t\text{ preserves the matched binders, scrutinee and position}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{iflet}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement is conditional on the ordinary opaque-Rust premises.

\subsection{Stratify: Source to Strata}
Construct the deterministic dependency SCC order. Independently check both
contracts and exact source preservation; the target need only satisfy the
admissible component contract, not reproduce that deterministic order.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Stratify}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Strata}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{source}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{stratify}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement follows from freezing lower components, subject to the common
termination and Rust premises.

\subsection{Incremental: Strata to Incremental}
Construct fresh delta declarations and the default covering expansion.
Independently check exact base preservation, erasure, coverage and substitutions;
do not compare target names or variant order with a regenerated expansion.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Incremental}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Incremental}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{strata}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{incremental}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The total finite refinement uses DL-Delta and DL-FrozenNegation; partial Rust
requires the additional prefix-safety obligation, not merely this check.

\subsection{Storage: Incremental to Storage}
Record the existing deterministic concrete providers, initializers, layouts,
bindings and maintenance. Independently check exact base preservation and the
target's admissible storage decisions, without reconstructing the default plan
or whitelisting concrete Rust types.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Storage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{incremental}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{storage}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement requires the stated $\alpha$ laws for the selected providers.

\subsection{Fork: Storage to Round}
Construct bound CQ holes and explicit operational records, copying provider
types, initializers and layout maintenance exactly. Carry a sub-pipeline and
typed interface for each hole and the configuration's complete pull-printer
allowlist. Independently check variant-to-hole correspondence, frozen-key
construction, storage preservation, terminal declarations, publication
dependencies and coverage, termination and output references. Alternative
publication permutations or loop forms need not equal Fork's default output.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Fork}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Round}}(t)\quad
 \mathrm{storage}(t)=s}{\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{fork}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Refinement is a storage-state simulation with the same partial-Rust premises.

\subsection{Print rounds: Round to Rust}
The sequential terminal printer interprets recorded bindings, provider types,
initializers, admission and publication operations, loop and termination
records, observers and output conversions, plus one nested CQ macro invocation
per hole. It never prints joins or substitutes a default choice for a supplied
record. CQ's selected terminal printer expands each hole later. Shared Datalog
printing operations live in \code{cq-datalog-print}; extension printers reuse
them through phase evaluation and alternative publication realizations.
The default realization and expansion remain unchanged.
As with core terminal printers, there is no independent Rust equivalence
checker: contract checks, baseline expansion comparison and executable oracle
agreement supply evidence, not a general Rust refinement proof.

\subsection{Parallel partition: Round to extension-owned annotated Round}
The separate extension accepts an unannotated source with sharded admission,
checks both Round contracts, and requires exact erasure of the embedded target
Round to the source, including policies and per-hole CQ queues. It records
exactly one annotation per eligible rule scan, in component seed/step order,
selecting occurrence zero and strategy \code{rayon\_sharded}. The Round
certificate checks a fresh direct scan binding and one renamed leading
positive occurrence; negative key sets are not partitioned.
The extension requires vector-backed delta inputs and a native layout's
contiguous vector bucket for a key-zero scan. Opaque aliases and non-vector
containers remain admissible in base Storage but are not supported by this
extension.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-Partition}{\mathrm{erase}(t.\mathrm{round})=s\quad
 s\text{ is unannotated, with certified admission and native layout providers}\quad
 t\text{ has the exact ordered scan certificates}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{partition}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The printer partitions the same immutable leading scan into indexed Rayon
chunks with grain $\max(g,\lfloor n/(4\,\mathrm{threads})\rfloor)$, where
$g$ is the recorded minimum grain (default 64). Inline evaluation requires
both total member delta size and the sum of driving scan lengths over holes
to be below the recorded threshold (default 1024); DOOP selects 64 explicitly.
A one-worker pool also evaluates inline. Thus a small delta cannot serialize
an expensive full-input hole. This avoids narrow-delta task overhead without changing
the selected occurrence or CQ rule. A different occurrence would require a
new checked rule; it is not an unchecked performance choice.

\subsection{Sharded admission and exclusive-layout publication (extension)}
The extension's Storage rewrite replaces only canonical IDB membership;
full/delta layout maps, all other Storage records and CQ queues are unchanged.
A Round-to-extension pass records the small-work threshold, partition grain,
admission shard-count policy and ownership/publication certificate. None of
these parallel choices belongs to the base language or default expansion.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{CK-DL-ShardStorage}{\mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(s)\quad
 \mathrm{WF}_{\mathrm{Storage}}(t)\quad
 t\text{ changes only canonical IDB membership providers}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{shardStorage}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
Let $S$ be a power of two at least the worker count and let
$h_S(x)=h(x)\mathbin{\&}(S-1)$. Canonical admission uses the full tuple:
$C_i=\{r\in C\mid h_S(r)=i\}$, so equal tuples always share a shard.
Each shard owns its \code{seen} set exclusively and inserts candidates once;
its newly admitted rows form $N_i$. Concatenation is in shard order, but
output order is unspecified. The termination condition is any admitted row.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-ShardedAdmission}{N_i=C_i\setminus A_i\quad A'_i=A_i\cup C_i
 \quad 0\leq i<S}
 {\mathrm{set}(\mathrm{concat}_{i=0}^{S-1}N_i)=C\setminus A}
\end{mathpar}
Each native layout has exactly one owner task during publication. Full layouts
extend their previous state; after all full-layout tasks join, relation
containers extend and deltas swap. A second scope replaces each delta map with
its recorded default provider and rebuilds it, including empty deltas, then joins before evaluation.
The original \code{IndexRead}/\code{Rows} implementations serve CQ holes
without fallback index construction or a shard-routing hash on every probe.
\begin{mathpar}
\Rule{DL-LayoutPublication}{\mathrm{owner}\text{ is a bijection on layouts}\quad
 L'_\ell=L_\ell\cup\mathrm{layout}_\ell(N)\quad
 \text{all full owners join before delta replacement}}
 {\forall\ell.\ \alpha(L'_\ell)=\alpha(L_\ell)\cup\mathrm{layout}_\ell(N)}
\Rule{CK-DL-ShardOwnership}{
 \{[i,i+1)\mid 0\leq i<S\}\text{ disjointly covers admission shards}\quad
 \mathrm{owner}\text{ is total and injective on each full/delta layout domain}\quad
 \text{every required layout completes before evaluation}}
 {\mathrm{check}_{\mathrm{shardedRound}}(s,t)}
\end{mathpar}
The checker validates admission's symbolic shard ownership and whole-layout
owner bijections directly, without invoking the certificate producer. It checks
complete membership/coverage and each stratum's publication barriers. The printer
uses certified layout lists and owner labels to generate and order its tasks.
Reordered certificates and valid owner permutations are accepted. Scoped tasks
receive exclusive mutable seen shards or native maps, never locks. Below
the recorded candidate threshold, admission runs inline while preserving shard
concatenation order; publication similarly avoids tasks for small next sets.
Purity, lawful hashing/equality/cloning and the partial-Rust prefix premises
remain required. Neither ownership checking nor the counters regression is a
general Rust equivalence proof.
The base printing library exports generic \code{full\_insert},
\code{all\_extend}, \code{delta\_swap} and \code{delta\_rebuild} generators.
Both terminals reuse those operations; the extension changes only their
grouping into checked phases. Delta rebuilds use default replacement in both
terminals, preserving the base expansion rather than silently using \code{clear}.
The extension printer's default-off \code{hole-timing} feature emits per-hole
and per-chunk evaluation times, input/candidate counts, partition status and
the erased CQ rule text. No timers, atomics or logging are emitted without
that feature. Chunk time excludes record emission; hole wall time includes
chunk-record output, so diagnostic times are not performance benchmark times.

\paragraph{Measured design choice.}
The initial 1024-row minimum grain left the dominant cast/points-to hole with
only about 3.2 chunks per round in avrora and 4.3 in xalan. At 15 workers,
the maximum chunk accounted for about 50\% and 33\% of its aggregate work,
respectively. The same leading occurrence can therefore be split more finely;
these observations do not establish a memory-bandwidth limit.
The rejected sharded-layout prototype added a routing hash before each native
map hash. A corrected fast-hash prototype with threshold 64 took 1.052, 1.916,
2.779 and 1.783 seconds in phase-only runs for cassandra, avrora, xalan and
tomcat at 15 workers. The selected native-layout hybrid took 0.860, 1.684,
2.529 and 1.161 seconds respectively, retaining sharded admission while
removing the extra probe hash. These are diagnostic phase-only observations,
not the isolated uninstrumented acceptance timings. The hybrid's counters
regression observes 22000 native probes in both sequential and parallel
executions and zero fallback builds. The rejected index provider is removed,
not retained as an unmeasured default or compatibility path.
